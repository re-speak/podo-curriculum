# English Freetalking — Topics

**11 themes · ~120 topics × 2 levels · growing.** One topic = one session.

**The theme list is deliberately the same as `korean/tracks/4-freetalking`.** The two curricula
share a learner and should share their best topics; a tutor who teaches both sees one inventory,
and a topic that works in one language almost always works in the other. Every topic below is
marked with what it shares:

- **`KR n-m`** — the same topic as Korean theme *n*, topic *m*. Ported, not reinvented.
- **`NEW → KR`** — written here first. Candidates to propose back to Korean; see
  [`proposals-to-korean.md`](./proposals-to-korean.md).
- **`EN-only`** — deliberately does not transfer, with the reason given.

*Rebuilt from the full Korean track; English title, naturalness and ownership review updated
2026-08-14. All Core references derived against the current 122-lesson spine.*

## The failure mode is not a boring topic — it is a topic with no answer

> **"What does success mean to you?"** reads well in a table of contents. Ask it and an adult cannot
> produce a first sentence within three seconds **in their own language**, let alone in English.
> **"What's the best thing you've bought this year?"** looks shallow, the answer arrives instantly,
> and the answer is a *story* — which is what keeps a 25-minute session moving.

### Titles should name a conversation, not label a category

The title is catalog language, so it does not have to duplicate the tutor's opening question. It
does need to sound as though it was written in English. Avoid bare evaluations or translated
category labels such as `Worth the money`, `Overrated` or `My clothes`: they leave the person,
experience or decision unstated and can read like review tags or advertising copy.

Prefer one of three shapes:

- a personal noun phrase: `A purchase that was worth it`;
- a concrete angle: `What you really do when you're stressed`;
- a natural choice or question: `More money or more time?`.

Use `you` for the learner-facing catalog rather than making the title speak as an invented `I`.
Keep the title shorter than the opening, but never achieve brevity by deleting the human subject of
the conversation.

### The three-second test applies to the **first rung**, not to the title

This is the correction that shaped this rewrite. An earlier version of this file tested topic
*titles* and threw away anything abstract. Reading Korean's built decks showed that is the wrong
target. `07-untranslatable-words` should fail on its title — nobody can name an untranslatable word
on demand — but its ladder opens with *"learned any new words lately?"*, which anyone answers
instantly, and walks up to the hard question from there.

So a title may be abstract **if its opening question is not.** Every topic below therefore states
its `Opens:` line — the sentence the tutor actually says first — and that line is what the
three-second test is run against.

### The three tests, before a topic goes on the list

1. **Does the learner start their first sentence within three seconds of the `Opens:` line?**
2. **Can you dig into that answer with "why" at least three more times?**
3. **Does the tutor have their own answer?** Without one it is a survey, not a conversation.

**Discard when** the answer ends at "yeah, true", or when one side ends up lecturing. A topic with a
predetermined right answer becomes a sermon — which is why *what you do for the environment* was
removed from this list **and from Korean's**, rather than reframed. Every other rejected candidate
was kept; that one could not be made into a conversation between equals.

### Four more rules the topic list obeys

- **Experience beats opinion.** Abstract topics carry a vocabulary tax — discussing work-life
  balance needs abstract nouns a B2 learner may not have, so the session stalls on lexis rather
  than ideas. Experience topics run on verbs the learner already owns.
- **"Lately" beats "in general".** A topic with 요즘 / recently / this week attached is guaranteed to
  have material. An abstract topic assumes the learner arrived with a prepared opinion.
- **Make places where the learner is the expert.** A Japanese learner and a foreign tutor are each
  half-expert. On Theme 1 the learner is the one *teaching*, in English — which is the most valuable
  thing that can happen in a freetalking hour, and the reason that theme leads the list.
- **Funny topics are load-bearing.** Thirty earnest sessions is a course nobody renews. Theme 4 is
  not filler; it is what makes the track survive contact with a real learner, and it is where you go
  to rescue a session that has gone heavy.

### `深く` — heavy topics are delayed, not deleted

Family, breakups, regret and turning points are good topics and wrong for session two with a tutor
you have just met. **`深く` does not mean cut it. It means bring it out once a relationship exists.**

Every topic remains optional, including topics without `深く`. The learner may say **"Let's do a
different one"** without explaining why, and the tutor switches topics immediately. Bags, money,
relationships, work problems, embarrassing memories and hypothetical emergencies can all be
personal even when the question looks light.

## The four formats

The format decides how the session is driven, so each topic carries its tag.

| Format | How it runs | The tutor's second slot holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **`story`** | pull out one experience, then spend the rest of the time on detail | the tutor's own **30-second experience** |
| **`choose`** | make them pick inside three seconds, then spend the rest on *why* | the tutor's pick and a one-line reason |
| **`両国`** | learner explains their side → tutor explains theirs → both are surprised | the tutor's side of the same question |
| **`opinion`** | position → grounds → tutor argues the other side to test it | a short **model position** to borrow |

**A `story` topic must never be given a model opinion.** Hand a learner an opinion to copy and they
will relay it instead of telling their own story. The tutor's 30-second experience is the model —
it demonstrates length and shape without supplying content.

## The four regions of a topic page

| Region | What |
| --- | --- |
| **Opening question** | one line the tutor reads aloud. Not the title — the sentence that gets said |
| **Model** | `opinion` → a short position · `story` → the tutor's 30-second experience |
| **Borrowed expressions** | **vocabulary only.** Articles, auxiliaries and inflections belong to Core |
| **Question ladder** | easy → spicy. Drop a rung if it collapses, climb one if it is rolling |

## The session

25 minutes. **The ladder is a content pool, not a timetable** — a fast learner reaches the top, a
slow talkative one stops halfway, and neither is behind. Do not cut an answer short to reach a rung.

| Segment | Time | What happens |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Warm-up | first 3–5 min | the `Opens:` question, to get the mouth moving |
| Main | the middle | climb the ladder as far as it goes |
| Close | last 2–3 min | re-say the most useful correction of the day, leave feedback |

## Two versions of every topic

Same theme, same question, same shape; only the **language load** differs.

| Version | How it is made |
| --- | --- |
| **B2–C1 · full** | Written **first**, unconstrained — the most natural way to run this topic |
| **B1 · accessible** | Derived from it: simplify the opening passage first, then change **only** the questions that genuinely block |

**The accessible version is not an abridgement.** Same facts, same positions, same depth of
thinking — cut those and the later rungs lose the anchor they refer back to.

**Entry floor: Core 53.** Below that the learner lacks the past, the perfect and the connectors a
story needs. Individual topics name a higher floor where they need one.

# Theme 1 · Between two countries · `両国`

*The track's flagship, and the one theme where the learner does the teaching. Both sides are
half-expert, so neither runs out of things to say.*

**Written country-neutral, on purpose.** Korean's version can name Korea because every Korean tutor
shares one other side. An English tutor could be from Manila, Lagos, Glasgow or Ohio, so no topic
here names a country — each asks about **where your tutor is from**. Two Korean topics did not
survive that constraint and are noted at the end of the theme.

**1. Something that surprised you about another culture** `両国`
*Opens:* "Tell me about a time something surprised you about another country—or about someone from
there." *(works for a learner who has never left Japan: a person, a film, a shop, a coworker)*
*Ladder:* what happened → what did you expect instead → where did that expectation come from → did
you say anything at the time → has it changed how you see it → what would surprise *me* about Japan
*Moves:* `I expected ___, but ___.` **(Core 56)** · `It turned out ___.` **(Core 73)** `chunk`
*Shared:* KR 1-1, rewritten so contact is not required

**2. What people often get wrong about Japan** `両国`
*Opens:* "What do people from other countries assume about Japan that isn't true?"
*Ladder:* what do they assume → where do they get it → what's the reality → what do *you* assume
about my country → have you ever been wrong about somewhere → what should people ask instead
*Moves:* `People think ___, but ___.` **(Core 56)** · `That's only true if ___.` **(Core 57)**
*Shared:* KR 1-2

**3. A Japanese food everyone should try** `両国`
*Opens:* "What food would you make me eat if I came to Japan?"
*Ladder:* what is it → what does it taste like → when and where is it best → who eats it with you →
what would you warn me about → how much can you change it for a foreigner before it stops counting
*Moves:* `You have to try ___.` **(Core 41)** · `It's best when ___.` **(Core 58)**
*Shared:* KR 1-3 · *one of the four strongest topics in the theme*

**4. A table manner that differs between countries** `両国`
*Opens:* "Do you pick your bowl up when you eat?" *(a yes/no the learner answers instantly, then the
tutor reveals their side — the contrast has to be shown before it can be discussed)*
*Ladder:* what else is fixed at your table → who starts → what would be rude → what do you do at my
country's table that would be rude at yours → who taught you → does anyone still care
*Moves:* `We always ___.` **(Core 12)** · `You're not supposed to ___.` `chunk`
*Shared:* KR 1-4

**5. A place you would show a visitor** `両国`
*Opens:* "If I visited for one day, where would you take me — not the famous place."
*Ladder:* where → why there → when did you find it → who have you taken → what would I not get about
it → where would you take me in *my* country if you could pick
*Moves:* `I'd take you to ___.` **(Core 66)** · `It's not famous, but ___.` **(Core 56)**
*Shared:* KR 1-5

**6. A word that doesn't translate neatly** `両国`
*Opens:* "Have you learned any new English words recently?" *(the title fails the three-second test;
this line does not, and it climbs to the title by rung three)*
*Ladder:* which word → what does it actually mean → what Japanese word has no English word → who do
you use it with → why does *who you're talking to* change it → is meaning enough if the feeling is lost
*Moves:* `There's no word for ___.` **(Core 7)** · `The closest is ___, but ___.` **(Core 56)**
*Shared:* KR 1-7 · *the proof that the three-second test belongs on the opening line*

**7. Something Japan does especially well** `choose`
*Opens:* "Convenience stores, trains, taxis, delivery — pick the one Japan does best."
*Ladder:* which and why → give me a scene → what does my country do better → what would you miss most
abroad → what's actually overrated → what would you change about the one you picked
*Moves:* `Nothing beats ___.` `chunk` · `To be fair, ___.` **(Core 93)**
*Shared:* KR 1-8 · format is `choose`, not `両国` — Korean's theme tag is loose here

**8. How you spend New Year's Day** `両国`
*Opens:* "What do you actually do on New Year's Day?"
*Ladder:* walk me through the day → what do you eat → who do you see → what has changed since you
were a child → what do you skip now → what happens in my country
*Moves:* `We usually ___.` **(Core 12)** · `We used to ___, but not anymore.` **(Core 75)**
*Shared:* KR 1-10 · seasonal-evergreen; leave it on the list all year

*Two Korean topics do not transfer.* **KR 1-6 workplace culture** moves to Theme 7 and is reworded
so students, homemakers and retirees can answer — Korean's own note in 일과 돈 asks for that and this
theme broke it. **KR 1-9 drama vs reality** is dropped `EN-only`: a Korean learner usually arrives
*through* K-content, but a Japanese adult studying English for work may consume none of it, so the
topic assumes exposure the learner may not have.

## Adding a topic

Write the title, then write the **opening line**, then answer that opening line out loud in your own
language and time it. Over three seconds and the opening is wrong — the title may still be fine.
Then run the three tests: three seconds, three "why"s deep, and the tutor has an answer.

Write the **B2–C1 version first**, unconstrained. Derive B1 by simplifying the opening passage and
changing only the questions that genuinely block. Do not shorten the thinking.

Three places to add every week:

- **Season and timing** — cherry blossom, rainy season, year-end, new year. When the season passes,
  leave the topic on the list. It comes back next year.
- **What people are talking about now** — a show everyone's watching, something new. **Not politics
  and not news events**: both become sermons or put the tutor in an impossible position.
- **What the learner raised themselves.** A topic the learner talked about at length has already
  passed all three tests. The tutor notes it and it goes on the list. **These are the strongest
  topics in the track.**

When a theme feels thin, do not pad it with topics — **open a new theme.** The ceiling on this track
is not the number of topics; it is the number of areas a learner wants to talk about.
