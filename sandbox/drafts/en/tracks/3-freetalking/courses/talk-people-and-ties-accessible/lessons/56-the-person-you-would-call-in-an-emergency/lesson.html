What an emergency contact represents (緊急時の連絡先が表すもの)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
An emergency call asks for more than sympathy.緊急時の電話では、共感だけでなく実際の助けも求めます。
The right contact depends on the kind of problem.誰に連絡するかは、問題の種類によって変わります。
A trusted person may know our needs but lack the right skill.信頼する人は自分を分かっていても、必要な技能がない場合があります。
A professional service may be the safest first call.専門サービスや緊急サービスが最初の連絡先として安全なことがあります。
After that, personal support can help with decisions and next steps.そのあと、身近な人が判断や次の行動を支えてくれます。
In the first few minutes, reliability can matter more than emotional closeness.最初の数分は、心の近さより、確実に対応してくれることが大切な場合があります。
Distance limits physical help, but advice, coordination, and reassurance can still reach us.距離があると直接の助けは限られますが、助言や手配、安心感は届けられます。
A move, illness, or relationship change can alter which people and services belong in a support network.引っ越し、病気、人間関係の変化で、支援体制に含まれる人やサービスも変わります。
Some people do not have one obvious personal contact, and a service may be part of the answer.すぐ思い浮かぶ身近な人がいなくても、サービスを選ぶ方法があります。
A support network of people and services is stronger when roles and contact details are clear before a crisis.緊急事態の前に役割や連絡先が分かっていると、人やサービスを含む支援体制はもっと確かになります。support network人やサービスを含む支援体制

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

全部答えなくても大丈夫です。一つの答えをゆっくり広げましょう。

First call (最初の連絡先)

WARM-UP 1If something went badly wrong tonight, who's the first person you'd call?もし今夜、大きな問題が起きたら、最初に誰に電話しますか？

Follow up
  • A safe hypothetical is enough; what kind of problem are you imagining?
  • If no person comes to mind, which service would you contact first?

Why them (その人を選ぶ理由)

WARM-UP 2Why would that person or service be the first choice?なぜその人、またはサービスを最初に選びますか？

Follow up
  • What could they do immediately?
  • What could they not do?

What you would say (伝えること)

QUESTION 1What information would you give first?最初にどんな情報を伝えますか？

Follow up
  • Which detail would help them act?
  • What could wait until later?

Needed before (過去の必要)

QUESTION 2Have you ever needed that kind of help—or can we keep it hypothetical?そのような助けが必要だったことはありますか、それとも仮定の話にしますか？

Follow up
  • If real, what kind of support mattered?
  • If hypothetical, which step seems hardest?

Someone calling you (自分に連絡する人)

QUESTION 3Is there anyone who might call you for help—or what role could you handle?助けを求めて自分に連絡する人はいますか、または、自分ならどんな役割を担えますか？

Follow up
  • What help could you realistically offer?
  • When would you call a professional instead?

Has it changed? (変わった連絡先)

QUESTION 4Has your first-choice contact changed over time?最初に連絡する相手は、時間とともに変わりましたか？

Follow up
  • If yes, what changed around you?
  • If not, what makes the arrangement stable?

Distance (距離)

QUESTION 5How much does distance matter in an emergency?緊急時に、距離はどのくらい重要ですか？

Follow up
  • Which help requires someone nearby?
  • Which help can still come from far away?

A stronger network (より強い支援体制)

QUESTION 6What could make a support network of people and services more reliable before an emergency?緊急事態の前に、人やサービスを含む支援体制をもっと確かにするには何ができますか？

Follow up
  • Which contact detail should be easy to find?
  • Which person or service needs a clear role?
