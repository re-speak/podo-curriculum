The value of useless facts (役に立たない事実の価値)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
A piece of personal trivia has no obvious utility in work or daily life.役に立たない個人的な事実には、仕事や日常生活での明らかな実用性がありません。utility実用性
Its lack of utility can make a conversation feel more personal and memorable.実用性がないからこそ、会話がより個人的で記憶に残るものになります。
Trivial details often reveal habits, tastes, and abandoned interests more clearly than a formal introduction.ささいな細部は、かしこまった自己紹介よりも、習慣や好み、以前の興味をはっきり表すことがあります。
Some personal trivia describes practical skills whose original usefulness has faded.個人的な雑学の中には、もともとの実用性が薄れた技能についてのものもあります。
Others survive only because a coincidence was strange enough to remember.奇妙で記憶に残る偶然だったからこそ残っているものもあります。
A good useless fact is harmless, concise, and revealing without becoming too private.よい役に立たない事実は、害がなく簡潔で、私的になりすぎずに人柄を表します。
They provide an easy conversational hook for a genuine follow-up question.そのような事実は、自然な追加質問につながる会話のきっかけになります。
Usefulness depends on context; what feels pointless to us may fascinate someone else.役立つかどうかは状況次第で、自分には無意味でも、ほかの人には魅力的かもしれません。
Useful facts advertise competence, while useless ones often reveal character.役に立つ事実が能力を示す一方で、役に立たない事実は人柄を表すことがよくあります。
One well-chosen oddity can make an otherwise predictable introduction feel genuinely human.一つのうまく選んだ変わった事実で、ありきたりな自己紹介にも人間味が生まれます。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

全部答えなくても大丈夫です。一つの答えをゆっくり広げましょう。

One useless fact (役に立たない事実)

WARM-UP 1Tell me one useless fact about yourself.自分についての役に立たない事実を一つ教えてください。

Follow up
  • If one comes to mind, how would you prove it?
  • If none does, what harmless fact could someone use as an example?

Since when (いつから？)

WARM-UP 2Say how long the real fact has been true—or how long the imagined one would have been true.実際の事実ならいつからそうなのか、想像した例ならいつからという設定なのかを話してください。

Follow up
  • Did it begin on purpose?
  • What was different before?

Who knows (知っている人)

QUESTION 1Who knows—or might know—that fact, if anyone?その事実を知っている人、または知っていそうな人がいるなら、誰ですか？

Follow up
  • If someone knows or might know, how could they find out?
  • If nobody does, why might it never come up?

Why useless (役に立たない理由)

QUESTION 2What makes you dismiss it as useless?なぜそれを役に立たないと思うのですか？

Follow up
  • Could it be useful in one unusual situation?
  • What makes it worth remembering anyway?

How it started (始まり)

QUESTION 3How was that fact discovered or learned—or how could it be?その事実はどのように分かったり身についたりしたか、または、どのような経緯が考えられますか？

Follow up
  • Was there—or could there be—a reason at the time?
  • Which part of the origin matters most?

A useful fact (役に立つ事実)

QUESTION 4Which fact about you has genuine practical value?自分について、本当に役に立つ事実は何ですか？

Follow up
  • When is it useful?
  • Who would benefit from knowing it?

Back to the useless fact (役に立たない事実に戻る)

QUESTION 5Returning to the original useless fact, would that real or imagined example genuinely surprise people?最初に挙げた役に立たない事実に戻ると、その実際の例、または想像した例を聞いて人は驚きそうですか？

Follow up
  • If it would, what makes it surprising?
  • If it would not, why might it sound ordinary to other people?

A memorable introduction (記憶に残る自己紹介)

QUESTION 6Would you use that same useless fact in a playful introduction?その同じ役に立たない事実を、楽しい自己紹介で使いたいですか？

Follow up
  • If you would, what follow-up would you hope to hear?
  • If you would not, what kind of fact would you use instead?
