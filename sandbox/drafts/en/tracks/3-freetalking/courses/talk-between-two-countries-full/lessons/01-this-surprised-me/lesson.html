Why small surprises matter (小さな驚きが大切な理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
People often picture another country long before they visit it, through films, social media, and stories from friends.人は実際に訪れるずっと前から、映画やSNS、友人の話を通して、ほかの国を思い描いていることがよくあります。
Those early pictures are often incomplete, but they can still feel surprisingly certain.そうした最初のイメージは不完全なことが多いのに、なぜか確かなもののように感じられます。
The surprise begins when ordinary routines do not match the image they brought with them.日常の習慣が、自分の中にあったイメージと合わないとき、驚きが生まれます。
A small experience—how people queue, share food, or speak to strangers—can stand out more than a famous landmark.列の作り方や食べ物の分け方、知らない人への話し方といった小さな体験が、有名な観光名所より強く印象に残ることがあります。
Because it is ordinary, the detail can feel personal and stay in our memory longer.日常の一部だからこそ、その細かな違いは身近に感じられ、長く記憶に残ることがあります。
At first, we may judge the difference by treating habits from home as the standard.最初は、自国の習慣を基準にして、その違いを判断してしまうことがあります。
That first judgment can be useful if it becomes a question rather than a final conclusion.最初の判断も、最終的な結論ではなく問いに変えられれば、役に立つことがあります。
But one encounter rarely represents a whole country; the same custom can vary by region, generation, or situation.しかし、一度の出会いが国全体を表すことはほとんどなく、同じ習慣でも地域や世代、状況によって異なります。
Talking with different people or seeing the same situation again can change our first explanation.違う人と話したり、同じ状況をもう一度見たりすると、最初の説明が変わることがあります。
Asking why something surprised us can reveal both the new culture and the assumptions we brought with us.なぜ驚いたのかを考えると、新しい文化だけでなく、自分が持ち込んだ思い込みも見えてきます。assumption思い込み
The comparison can also make familiar habits at home easier to notice.比べることで、自国の慣れ親しんだ習慣にも気づきやすくなります。
Over time, that surprise may become curiosity and change how we see familiar life at home.時間がたつと、その驚きは好奇心に変わり、慣れ親しんだ自国の日常の見え方まで変えることがあります。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

全部答える必要はありません。一つの答えが深くなったら、それが今日の成功です。

Your story (あなたの話)

WARM-UP 1Tell me about a time something surprised you about another country—or about someone from there.ほかの国、またはその国の人について、何かに驚いたときのことを教えてください。

Follow up
  • Did it happen in person, online, or through a story?
  • What surprised you most?
  • Had anything similar happened before?

One detail (一つの細部)

WARM-UP 2What small detail from that moment do you remember most clearly?そのときの小さなことで、いちばんはっきり覚えているものは何ですか？

Follow up
  • What exactly did you see, hear, or notice?
  • What made that detail stand out?

What happened? (何が起きた？)

QUESTION 1What happened from the beginning to the moment that surprised you?最初から驚いた瞬間まで、何が起きましたか？

Follow up
  • When did you realize something was different?
  • What happened immediately afterward?

Your expectation (予想していたこと)

QUESTION 2What had you expected to happen instead?その代わりに、何が起きると思っていましたか？

Follow up
  • Which part of your expectation was most different from reality?
  • Did the difference feel positive, negative, or simply unfamiliar?
  • Did anyone else you know expect the same thing?

Where it came from (予想の出どころ)

QUESTION 3Where do you think that expectation came from?その予想はどこから来たと思いますか？

Follow up
  • Was it shaped more by media, another person, or an earlier experience?
  • What made that source seem believable?
  • Had you come across a different view before?

In the moment (その場で)

QUESTION 4How did you react at the time?そのとき、どう反応しましたか？

Follow up
  • Did you show your surprise or keep it to yourself?
  • What happened because of your response?
  • Would you respond differently now?

What changed? (何が変わった？)

QUESTION 5How has that experience changed how you see the country or its people?その経験で、その国や人々への見方はどう変わりましたか？

Follow up
  • What do you notice now that you might have missed before?
  • What part of your original view has not changed?
  • Has the change affected how you approach similar situations?

Turn it around (逆に考えよう)

QUESTION 6What part of everyday life in Japan might surprise someone visiting for the first time?日本を初めて訪れる人は、日常生活のどんなところに驚くと思いますか？

Follow up
  • Where would they be most likely to notice it?
  • What might they wrongly expect instead?
  • What should they understand before they judge it?
