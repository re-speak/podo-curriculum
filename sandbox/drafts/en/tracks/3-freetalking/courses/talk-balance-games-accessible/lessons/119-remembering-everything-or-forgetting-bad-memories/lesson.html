Remembering everything or forgetting bad memories? (すべて覚える、それとも嫌な記憶を忘れる？)

Let's compare remembering everything and forgetting bad memories, then explain what could change your choice. Please read the title aloud.すべてを覚えていることとつらい記憶を忘れることを比べ、何が変われば選択も変わるか話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

What makes the choice change (選択を変えるもの)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article.
Sleep helps some memories become stronger while others fade.睡眠中に強くなる記憶もあれば、薄れる記憶もあります。
People often remember the meaning of an event better than its exact words or timing.人は、出来事の正確な言葉や順番より、意味をよく覚えていることがあります。
A memory trigger can bring back a feeling before the full event is clear.記憶のきっかけによって、出来事全体が分かる前に感情が戻ることがあります。memory trigger記憶のきっかけ
Writing and photographs can preserve useful details without perfect recall.文章や写真なら、完全な記憶がなくても役立つ細部を残せます。
Attention decides which parts of an event are likely to enter memory at all.そもそも何を記憶するかは、そのとき何に注意を向けたかで変わります。
Painful memories can feel vivid even when ordinary details fade.つらい記憶は、普通の細部が薄れても鮮明に感じられることがあります。
Forgetting can protect attention, but it can also remove useful lessons.忘れることは心を守りますが、役立つ学びも失うことがあります。
Forgetting some details can make the main pattern or lesson easier to see.細かいことを一部忘れると、全体のパターンや学びが見えやすくなることがあります。
Remembering everything would also mean keeping every mistake and embarrassment clear.すべて覚えるなら、失敗や恥ずかしい出来事も鮮明に残ります。
A good choice would protect useful memories while helping people live with painful ones.よい選択は、役立つ記憶を守りながら、つらい記憶とともに生きる人を支えるものです。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of the conversation.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。会話の中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Use these pages as a flexible pool. Ask one question, react naturally, and share a brief real answer or relevant perspective of your own when it adds to the exchange. Skip freely and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

A memory from a photo (写真で戻る記憶)

WARM-UP 1What kind of happy memory can a photograph bring back?写真を見ると、どんな楽しい記憶が戻ることがありますか？

Follow up
  • Which detail returns first?
  • Which brings back more: a photo or a song?

A small moment to keep (残したい小さな出来事)

WARM-UP 2Which small ordinary moment is worth remembering for years?長く覚えていたい小さな出来事は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why could that ordinary moment matter later?
  • Would you record an ordinary day in a diary?

What to remember (覚えておくもの)

QUESTION 1Would you rather remember every ordinary detail or be able to forget painful memories?日常の細かいことをすべて覚えるのと、つらい記憶を忘れられるのと、どちらを選びますか？

Follow up
  • How would your choice help in daily life?
  • Which side could also cause problems?

What perfect memory makes easier (完全な記憶で楽になること)

QUESTION 2What would perfect memory make easier?完全な記憶があれば、何が楽になりますか？

Follow up
  • Could too much detail become distracting?
  • Would perfect memory help more at work or while studying?

Every embarrassment stays (恥ずかしさも残る)

QUESTION 3Would you want perfect memory if every embarrassment stayed vivid too?恥ずかしいことも鮮明に残るなら、完全な記憶がほしいですか？

Follow up
  • Would embarrassing memories get easier with time?
  • Which would bother you more: old mistakes or useless details?

Memory and arguments (記憶と口論)

QUESTION 4Would perfect memory make arguments easier or harder?何でも覚えていたら、口論は解決しやすくなりますか、それとも難しくなりますか？

Follow up
  • Would remembering the exact words help?
  • Is it sometimes better to forget a small mistake?

A mistake to forget (忘れたほうがよい失敗)

QUESTION 5What kind of small mistake is better to forget?どんな小さな失敗なら、忘れたほうがよいですか？

Follow up
  • Would forgetting help someone move on?
  • Is there still a lesson worth keeping?

Choosing what to forget (忘れる記憶を選ぶこと)

QUESTION 6Would you trust yourself to choose which memories to forget?どの記憶を忘れるか、自分で決めることを信頼できますか？

Answer first, then follow up
  • What could go wrong?
  • Would you wait a month before deciding?
