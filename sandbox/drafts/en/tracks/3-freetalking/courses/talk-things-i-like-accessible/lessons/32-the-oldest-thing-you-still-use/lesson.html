The oldest thing you still use (今も使っているいちばん古いもの)

Let's tell the life story of something that still works. Please read the title aloud.今も使える古い物の歩みを話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

What makes an object last (物が長く使える理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
An old object survives because someone keeps choosing to use it.古い物が残るのは、誰かが繰り返し使うことを選ぶからです。
Durable materials and simple construction can make repair easier.丈夫な材料と単純な作りは、修理をしやすくします。durable丈夫な
Familiarity also matters because we already know exactly how the object behaves.使い慣れた物は、どう動くかがよく分かっているので大切です。
Replacing it may require learning a new system for very little benefit.買い替えると、わずかな利点のために新しい使い方を覚える必要があるかもしれません。
An old tool may be less efficient but more dependable in a familiar task.古い道具は効率が低くても、慣れた作業では頼りになることがあります。
Repairs create a record of how the object and its owner adapted.修理の跡は、物と持ち主がどう対応したかという記録になります。
Keeping everything old is different from keeping what still serves a purpose.古い物を何でも残すことと、今も役立つ物を残すことは違います。
Some items are built to last, while others are difficult or expensive to repair.長持ちするように作られた物もあれば、修理が難しい物や高くつく物もあります。built to last長持ちするように作られた
The oldest useful thing in a home can reveal changing technology and habits.家で今も使ういちばん古い物から、技術や日々の習慣の変化が見えます。
What we refuse to replace shows which qualities new products have not improved.買い替えたくない物は、新しい製品でも改善できていない大切な性質を教えてくれます。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Your oldest useful thing (いちばん古い実用品)

WARM-UP 1What's the oldest thing you still use?今も使っているいちばん古い物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Where do you keep it?
  • What does it look like now?

Old versus new (古い物と新しい物)

WARM-UP 2What old item works better than its newer version?新しい物よりよく使える古い物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Which design choice makes it better?
  • What advantage does the newer version have?

How old (古さ)

QUESTION 1How can you guess an object's age just by looking at it?物を見ただけで、その古さはどう分かりますか？

Follow up
  • Does wear make the object better or worse?
  • Which materials age well?

Better with age (使うほど美しくなる物)

QUESTION 2What old object becomes more beautiful after years of use?何年も使うことで、より美しくなる古い物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Which signs of age add character?
  • When does wear stop being attractive?

Built to last (長く使える理由)

QUESTION 3Why do some things last while others break quickly?長く使える物と、すぐ壊れる物があるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • How much does repairable design matter?
  • Can a cheap object still be built to last?

Keep or let go? (残す？手放す？)

QUESTION 4Do you usually keep things or get rid of them quickly?物を取っておくほうですか、それともすぐに手放すほうですか？

Follow up
  • What rule guides your decision?
  • When has your habit caused a problem?

Repair or replace (修理か買い替えか)

QUESTION 5When do you repair something instead of replacing it?物を買い替えず、修理するのはどんなときですか？

Follow up
  • Which matters more: the cost or your feelings about the item?
  • What should companies make easier to repair?

Still useful in ten years (十年後も使う物)

QUESTION 6Which objects made today could still be useful in ten years?今作られている物の中で、十年後も役立ちそうなのは何ですか？

Follow up
  • What would help them last?
  • Which could last longer: a tool, furniture, or clothing?
