Why tiny things get to us (ささいなことが気になる理由)

A tiny irritation can become more annoying when it happens again and again.ささいないら立ちでも、何度も繰り返されると、いっそう気になります。irritationいら立ち
We notice it quickly because we already expect it to happen.また起きると思っているので、すぐに気づきます。
The problem may feel bigger when fixing it would be easy.簡単に直せそうなことほど、大きな問題のように感じることがあります。
Shared spaces create many small conflicts about noise, order, and timing.共有する場所では、音や片づけ、タイミングをめぐる小さな衝突が多く生まれます。
Other people may honestly not notice the thing that bothers us.自分が気になることに、ほかの人は本当に気づいていない場合があります。
Saying something can solve the problem, but it can also sound too serious.伝えれば解決するかもしれませんが、大げさに聞こえることもあります。
A joke can make a small complaint easier to hear.冗談を交えると、ささいな不満も受け止めてもらいやすくなります。
Our own habits probably annoy other people too.自分の習慣も、きっとほかの人を困らせています。
Stress and tiredness can make a tiny annoyance feel much worse.ストレスや疲れがあると、ささいなことがずっと気になりやすくなります。
A useful question is whether the problem needs action or just patience.大切なのは、行動が必要なのか、少し我慢すればよいのかを考えることです。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

全部答えなくても大丈夫です。一つの答えをゆっくり広げましょう。

One tiny annoyance (ささいな不満)

WARM-UP 1What's something small that irritates you that nobody else notices?ほかの人は気づかないのに、自分は気になるささいなことは何ですか？

Follow up
  • If one comes to mind, where does it happen?
  • If none does, which familiar or imagined complaint could you use—for example, loud typing?

The last time (直近の場面)

WARM-UP 2Say when you last noticed or heard about the real issue—or when the imagined one could happen.実際の問題なら最後に気づいたり話を聞いたりしたのはいつか、想像した問題ならいつ起こりそうかを話してください。

Follow up
  • What happened—or could happen?
  • Why did—or could—the detail stand out?

What gets to someone (気になる点)

QUESTION 1Which exact part seems most irritating, and to whom?具体的にどの部分が、誰にとっていちばん気になりそうですか？

Follow up
  • Would a small change solve it?
  • Could different people react differently?

Say something? (伝える？)

QUESTION 2Say whether anyone affected by the real issue spoke up—or what a person in the imagined case might say.実際の問題なら困っている人は何か伝えたのか、想像した例ならその人は何と言いそうかを話してください。

Follow up
  • If someone spoke about the real issue, what did they say?
  • If the case is imagined—or nobody spoke—what might keep someone silent?

Who agrees (同意する人)

QUESTION 3For the real issue, does anyone else agree—or in the imagined case, might other people agree?実際の問題ならほかにも同意する人はいますか、想像した例なら同意する人はいそうですか？

Follow up
  • If someone agrees or might agree, what would they notice?
  • If nobody agrees or seems likely to, why could people's reactions differ?

Your annoying habit (自分の気になる癖)

QUESTION 4What small thing might you do that annoys other people?自分がしていて、ほかの人を困らせるかもしれないささいなことは何ですか？

Follow up
  • If something comes to mind, has anyone mentioned it?
  • If nothing does, what habit would you most want someone to tell you about?

Changing over time (年齢と変化)

QUESTION 5Do small annoyances seem to get stronger with age?ささいな不満は、年齢とともに強くなると思いますか？

Follow up
  • If they do, what might change in people's patience?
  • If they do not, what might people learn to ignore?

Worth fixing (直す価値)

QUESTION 6Which tiny annoyance is actually worth fixing?ささいな不満のうち、本当に直す価値があるのはどれですか？

Follow up
  • What would be the smallest useful action?
  • Which annoyance is better left alone?

Today's feedback (今日のフィードバック)

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
