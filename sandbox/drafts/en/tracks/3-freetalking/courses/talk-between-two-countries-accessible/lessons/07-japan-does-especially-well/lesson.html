Something Japan does especially well (日本が特に得意なこと)

Today, we'll choose one everyday service Japan does especially well and explain why. Please read the title aloud.今日は、日本が特に得意な日常サービスを一つ選び、その理由を話します。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

What does “best” mean? (「一番」の意味)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Japan is famous for everyday services such as convenience stores, trains, taxis, and delivery.日本は、コンビニ、電車、タクシー、宅配などの日常サービスで有名です。
The best one depends on what a person needs at that moment.どれが一番かは、そのときに何が必要かで変わります。
Trains are useful because they come often and usually arrive when people expect them.電車は本数が多く、たいてい予想した時間に来るので便利です。
Convenience stores offer food, bill payment, tickets, and package services in one place.コンビニでは、食べ物、支払い、チケット、荷物のサービスを一か所で利用できます。
Taxis are usually clean and reliable, but their price can be high.タクシーはたいてい清潔で信頼できますが、料金が高いことがあります。reliable信頼できる
Delivery can save time because people can choose when and how to receive a package.宅配では荷物を受け取る時間や方法を選べるので、時間を節約できます。
Visitors may notice speed, while people who live in Japan may value getting the same good service every time.旅行者は速さに気づき、日本で暮らす人は毎回同じよいサービスを受けられることを大切にするかもしれません。
A very useful service can become something people expect everywhere.とても便利なサービスは、どこにでもあると期待するものになることがあります。
Good service also depends on workers, transport, and money that customers do not always see.よいサービスは、利用者にはいつも見えない働く人、交通、費用にも支えられています。
Even a good system has problems, and some services may be overrated.よい仕組みにも問題があり、評価されすぎているサービスもあるかもしれません。overrated評価されすぎた
A comparison is clearer when we talk about one real situation.一つの実際の場面について話すと、比較が分かりやすくなります。
The service we choose often shows what matters to us in daily life.私たちが選ぶサービスには、日常生活で何を大切にしているかが表れます。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Your pick (あなたの一番)

WARM-UP 1Convenience stores, trains, taxis, or delivery—pick the one Japan does best.コンビニ、電車、タクシー、宅配の中で、日本が一番得意だと思うものを選んでください。

Follow up
  • What was the first example that came to mind?
  • How often do you personally use it?
  • What would make you choose a different one?

A real scene (実際の場面)

WARM-UP 2Imagine needing that service on a busy day. What problem should it solve?忙しい日にそのサービスが必要だと想像してください。どんな問題を解決してほしいですか？

Follow up
  • What would you need at that moment?
  • Which detail would make the help feel reliable?
  • What inconvenience could the service prevent?

Why it wins (一番の理由)

QUESTION 1Why is your choice better than the other three?選んだものは、なぜほかの三つより優れていますか？

Follow up
  • Is speed, trust, price, or convenience the main reason?
  • Who gets the most help from it?
  • What could still be better?

For a visitor (旅行者にとって)

QUESTION 2When would a visitor find that service most useful?日本を訪れる人は、どんなときにそのサービスが一番役立つでしょうか？

Follow up
  • What might be difficult at first?
  • What should the visitor know before using it?
  • What part would be hard to find elsewhere?

Another country (ほかの国)

QUESTION 3What does another country do better in one of these services?この中のサービスで、ほかの国のほうが上手にしていることは何ですか？

Follow up
  • What is different there?
  • Would that way work in Japan?
  • What is the disadvantage?

Living abroad (海外で暮らすなら)

QUESTION 4Which of these services would you miss most if you lived abroad?海外で暮らすなら、この中で一番恋しくなるサービスはどれですか？

Follow up
  • Which part of your routine would change first?
  • What substitute would you probably use?
  • How long would it take you to adjust?

Overrated? (評価されすぎ？)

QUESTION 5Which Japanese service may get a little too much praise?日本のサービスの中で、少しほめられすぎているものはどれですか？

Follow up
  • What problem do people forget to mention?
  • Who would disagree with you?
  • What improvement would make the praise fair?

Make it better (もっとよくする)

QUESTION 6What would you change about the service you picked at the beginning?最初に選んだサービスを、どのように変えたいですか？

Follow up
  • Who would benefit from that change?
  • What might the change cost or make harder?
  • Would it still be the best after that change?
