# Contextual English — Table of Contents

**2 practical areas · 10 courses · 60 standalone lessons.** Contextual English is the applied
half of the curriculum: learners use language from Core to complete recognisable travel and
business tasks. It begins at low-intermediate and rises to high-level, high-stakes communication.

| Practical area | Courses | Lessons | Entry floor | Highest band |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Travel English** | 5 | 30 | Core **47** | B2–C1 bridge |
| **Business English** | 5 | 30 | Core **47** | B2–C1 bridge |

*Rebuilt 2026-08-13 around the two clearest learner needs. Everyday and social language belongs
inside Travel or Business when it serves a concrete task; otherwise it remains in Core or
Freetalking. The retired show/season architecture and the interim four-area architecture must not
be restored. Stable CTX ids are retained, but titles and language serve the task rather than a
previous numbering scheme.*

---

## The boundary with Core

- **Core teaches the language system:** a pattern, its meaning, and how to vary it.
- **Contextual teaches task completion:** selecting and combining known language under realistic
  pressure to get an external result.

A Contextual lesson fails if its title names one task while its two patterns perform unrelated
jobs. The title, scene, can-do, both learner lines, partner reactions, and final transfer must all
point at the same outcome. Titles therefore name tasks, not attractive fragments of dialogue.

Every lesson stands alone. Course order raises language load and situational pressure, but never
creates plot prerequisites. The final transfer changes at least two details while preserving the
same communicative job.

## The five regions of a lesson

| Region | What | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ***場面*** | who · where · what must be achieved | the practical starting point |
| **Can-do** | one observable outcome | the pass condition |
| **2 lines + reactions** | two connected moves in the same task | production |
| ***Expressions*** | fixed language needed for that task | production support |
| ***Understand*** | likely replies, announcements, or questions | reception only |

Core numbers show where the language system is taught. A course floor is an entry recommendation,
not a lock. A pattern more than about twelve Core lessons beyond the floor is marked `chunk` and
taught as bounded language for the situation.

# Area 1 · Travel English

Travel courses move from routine arrival tasks to disruptions, independent social travel, and
complex cases involving competing constraints. Social interaction appears here only when it helps
the learner travel successfully.

## Course 1 · Airports, arrivals and getting around · 6 lessons · **A2–B1** · floor: Core 47

**1. Check in and request a seat**

*場面: at an airline desk, 私 needs two seats together and prefers a window.*
*Can-do: complete check-in and request a suitable seat.*
- 私: "We'd like two seats together, if possible." — `We'd like ___, if possible.` **(Core 22)**
  → Agent: "I can do 24A and B. Is that all right?"
- 私: "Could we have a window seat?" — `Could we have ___?` **(Core 20)**
  → Agent: "24A is the window. You're all set."
- *Expressions:* `aisle seat` · `bags to check`
- *Understand:* `May I see your passport?` · `Boarding starts at 3:40.`

**2. Answer immigration questions clearly**

*場面: at passport control, an officer asks why 私 is visiting and where 私 will stay.*
*Can-do: give concise, consistent entry information.*
- 私: "I'm here for a two-week holiday." — `I'm here for ___.` `chunk`
  → Officer: "And where are you staying?"
- 私: "I'm staying at the Central Hotel." — `I'm staying at ___.` **(Core 29)**
  → Officer: "Thank you. Enjoy your trip."
- *Expressions:* `return ticket` · `travelling for work`
- *Understand:* `What's the purpose of your visit?` · `Anything to declare?`

**3. Confirm a number you did not hear**

*場面: at an airport information desk, 私 is unsure whether the bus leaves at 2:40 or 2:14.*
*Can-do: repair a misunderstood time or number before acting on it.*
- 私: "Sorry, could you say the departure time again?" — `Sorry, could you say ___ again?` **(Core 24)**
  → Staff: "Two forty."
- 私: "Did you say two-forty or two-fourteen?" — `Did you say ___ or ___?` **(Core 19)**
  → Staff: "Two-forty—four-zero."
- *Expressions:* `just to confirm` · `four-zero`

**4. Choose the right transport ticket**

*場面: at a station counter, 私 must choose between a day pass and an express ticket.*
*Can-do: compare ticket options and select one.*
- 私: "Is there a cheaper option?" — `Is there a ___er option?` **(Core 45)**
  → Staff: "The day pass is cheaper if you make three trips."
- 私: "Which one is faster?" — `Which one is ___er?` **(Core 45)**
  → Staff: "The express, by about twenty minutes."
- *Expressions:* `one-way` · `day pass`

**5. Ask for directions and confirm the route**

*場面: outside a station, 私 needs to reach a museum without taking the wrong exit.*
*Can-do: obtain directions and check the critical step.*
- 私: "How do I get to the museum?" — `How do I get to ___?` **(Core 11)**
  → Local: "Take the north exit and then the number eight bus."
- 私: "Do I take the north exit first?" — `Do I ___ first?` **(Core 13)**
  → Local: "Exactly. The bus stop is across the road."
- *Expressions:* `across from` · `you can't miss it`

**6. Arrange transport to your accommodation**

*場面: at the airport taxi desk, 私 needs a car to a hotel and wants the price before agreeing.*
*Can-do: state a destination and confirm time and cost.*
- 私: "Could you take us to the Central Hotel?" — `Could you take us to ___?` **(Core 60)** `chunk`
  → Staff: "That's about forty minutes from here."
- 私: "About how much will it cost?" — `About how much will ___ cost?` **(Core 19)**
  → Staff: "Between forty and fifty euros, depending on traffic."
- *Expressions:* `fixed fare` · `depending on traffic`

## Course 9 · Negotiation and diplomatic language · 6 lessons · **B2** · floor: Core 101

**49. Reframe a problem more accurately**

*場面: in a partner meeting, the other side calls a short delay a project failure.*
*Can-do: replace an unhelpful characterisation with a more accurate one.*
- 私: "It's not so much a failure as a delay." — `It's not so much ___ as ___.` **(Core 89)**
  → Partner: "The delay still has a cost."
- 私: "I'd call it a setback rather than a fundamental problem." — `I'd call it ___ rather than ___.` **(Core 89)**
  → Partner: "That's fair, provided we recover quickly."

**50. Make a conditional proposal**

*場面: negotiating scope, 私 can accept an earlier date only if one feature is removed.*

- 私: "We could meet the earlier date, provided we reduce the scope." — `We could ___, provided ___.` `chunk`
  → Client: "What would you remove?"

**51. Push back on an unsafe commitment**

*場面: a stakeholder presses 私 to promise a date before a supplier has confirmed availability.*

**52. Explain a trade-off without oversimplifying it**

**53. Acknowledge a point without giving up your position**

## How a lesson passes

A learner passes by completing the named task with both patterns, not by reciting the model. The
final transfer changes at least two practical variables—such as the person, constraint, evidence,
or desired remedy—while preserving the same outcome. Reception targets are recognised, never
assessed as production. No lesson previews another lesson or assumes a shared story.
