## 1 · Get the meaning

Present a two- to four-turn exchange containing both patterns. Ask one meaning question that can be answered without explaining grammar.

## 2 · Pattern A

- Hear/read the model.
- Show the stable words and clearly mark the changeable slots. A slot may be a word, phrase, action, or clause when its load fits the level.
- Contrast one likely error only if it blocks meaning or transfer.
- Complete three supported substitutions and one personal sentence.

## 3 · Pattern B

- Repeat the same sequence.
- Make the relationship to Pattern A explicit: answer, contrast, follow-up, repair, or expansion.

## 4 · Put them together

Complete one information-gap, decision, or role-play exchange in which Pattern A naturally creates a need for Pattern B.

## 5 · Say it without the model

Remove the written sentences. The learner responds to two prompts, then performs the full can-do task once.

## 6 · Quick check

- **Understands:** selects the intended meaning of each pattern.
- **Controls:** changes the slot without breaking the frame.
- **Builds:** handles the language inside each slot at the expected level.
- **Uses:** completes the can-do task with both patterns.
- **Sounds clear:** preserves the lesson’s key stress or linking cue.

## Authoring guardrails

- Two patterns means two speakable, changeable frames. Fixed chunks may support the exchange, but they do not count as one of the two patterns.
- A slot does not have to be one word. Clause-sized slots become appropriate as learners gain the language needed to build them.
- Judge openness by level: constrain early slots tightly; allow later patterns to organize reasons, conditions, propositions, and arguments.
- `subject + base verb`, “irregular past forms,” and similar grammar formulas are metadata, not patterns.
- A frame must retain enough literal language to retrieve aloud. A slot-only skeleton such as `___ + ___ + ___` fails this requirement.
- Pair frames that cooperate as question/answer, request/response, statement/follow-up, problem/repair, or position/contrast.
- Do not turn vocabulary lists, exception lists, grammar notes, or sound cues into extra objectives.
- Keep the first model highly frequent and immediately useful.
- Recycle old language in new slots. New content words should normally be limited to six to eight.
- Keep grammar support subordinate: explain only the form choice needed to use the two patterns, then return to meaning and speaking.
- Add a sound cue only when it improves comprehension or delivery of the two frames; pronunciation has no separate syllabus.
- Teach contractions and ordinary spoken forms alongside full forms, not months later as “native speed.”
- Assess intelligibility and task success before accent imitation.
- Move from listening/recognition to controlled speaking to personal speaking.
