BETWEEN TWO COUNTRIES

A table manner that differs between countries (国によって違う食事のマナー)

Let's compare one table habit and the meaning people give it. Please read the title aloud.食卓での習慣を一つ比べ、その意味について話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

The hidden rules at a table (食卓にある見えないルール)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Table manners feel natural because people learn them long before they can explain them.食事のマナーは説明できるようになるずっと前から身につけるため、自然に感じられます。
A small action can therefore carry very different meanings in two countries.そのため、小さな動作が二つの国でまったく違う意味を持つことがあります。
Picking up a bowl may show good manners in one place and look unusual in another.お椀を持つことが、ある場所では礼儀正しく、別の場所では珍しく見えることがあります。
The order of eating, sharing dishes, and starting a meal can also follow quiet rules.食べる順番、料理の分け方、食べ始める時にも、暗黙のルールがあります。
Visitors often notice the rule only after someone reacts to them.旅行者は、誰かに反応されて初めてそのルールに気づくことがよくあります。
Embarrassment makes the moment memorable, but it does not always explain the reason.恥ずかしさでその瞬間は記憶に残りますが、理由まで分かるとは限りません。embarrassment恥ずかしさ
Asking who taught the habit can reveal family, age, and regional differences.その習慣を誰に教わったか聞くと、家庭、世代、地域の違いが見えてきます。
Some rules remain important while others are becoming flexible.今も大切なルールもあれば、柔軟になっているものもあります。
Knowing the rule is useful, but understanding its purpose makes adaptation easier.ルールを知ることは役立ちますが、目的まで分かると合わせやすくなります。adaptation適応
A respectful guest observes, asks, and avoids treating one table as a whole country.礼儀ある客は、よく見て、質問し、一つの食卓を国全体だと思わないようにします。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Your last meal with someone (最近の食事)

WARM-UP 1Who did you last eat a meal with?最近、誰と食事をしましたか？

Follow up
  • Where were you?
  • What did you eat?

At that table (その食卓で)

WARM-UP 2What do you always do at the table without thinking about it?食卓で、何も考えずにいつもしていることは何ですか？

Follow up
  • Do you say anything before you start?
  • What do you do with your bowl or your hands?

Normal at home (家では普通)

QUESTION 1What do you do at the table that feels completely normal to you?食卓で、自分にとってはまったく普通だと感じることは何ですか？

Follow up
  • Who taught you that?
  • Do your friends do it too?

Who starts (誰から始める)

QUESTION 2In your family, who starts eating first?あなたの家では、誰が最初に食べ始めますか？

Follow up
  • What do you say before eating?
  • Is it different in a restaurant?

A habit abroad (海外で気づいた習慣)

QUESTION 3What's one table habit you've noticed in another country?ほかの国の食事で、気づいた習慣を一つ挙げるとしたら何ですか？

Follow up
  • Did you notice it in person or online?
  • Would it feel natural in Japan?

Home and restaurant (家と店)

QUESTION 4Do you eat differently at home and in a restaurant?家と店では、食べ方が変わりますか？

Follow up
  • Which one is more relaxed?
  • What exactly changes?

Who taught you (誰に教わった)

QUESTION 5Who taught you your table manners?食事のマナーは、誰に教わりましたか？

Follow up
  • Were they strict about it?
  • Do you still follow all of it?

The opposite (逆に)

QUESTION 6Now the opposite — is there a table rule you were taught but don't follow?逆に、教わったけれど守っていない食事のルールはありますか？

Follow up
  • When did you stop?
  • Would you follow it in front of your family?
