Let's choose one goal and find a realistic first step. Please read the title aloud.目標を一つ選び、現実的な最初の一歩を考えましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

Turning a wish into a plan (願いを計画に変える)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
A deadline can help a vague wish become a real decision.期限があると、ぼんやりした願いが本当の決断になります。vague曖昧な
Choosing one goal means accepting that other goals may wait.目標を一つ選ぶことは、ほかの目標を待たせることでもあります。
We often delay a goal when the first step is unclear or too large.最初の一歩が分かりにくかったり大きすぎたりすると、目標を先延ばしにしがちです。
Breaking it into a small action makes progress easier to begin.小さな行動に分けると、始めやすくなります。
Time is not the only barrier; fear and uncertainty can also stop us.時間だけでなく、不安や自信のなさも行動を止めることがあります。
Giving up one old goal can create space for a more important one.古い目標を一つ手放すと、もっと大切な目標のための余裕ができます。
Telling someone can add support, but it can also add pressure.誰かに話すと支えになる一方、プレッシャーになることもあります。
The result may matter less than learning to take the next step consistently.結果よりも、次の一歩を続けて踏み出せるようになることが大切な場合もあります。
One action this week is more useful than a perfect plan for next month.来月からの完璧な計画より、今週の具体的な一歩のほうが役立ちます。
A good goal can change as we learn what is actually possible.本気の目標でも、可能なことが分かるにつれて変えてかまいません。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

One goal (一つの目標)

WARM-UP 1What's one thing you'd like to finish this year?今年中に終わらせたいことを一つ挙げるとしたら何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why does it matter to you?
  • What could make it difficult?

After it's finished (終えたあと)

WARM-UP 2Imagine finishing something important. What would change first?大切なことを終えたと想像してください。最初に何が変わりますか？

Follow up
  • Which change would matter most?
  • Would you celebrate or start the next goal?

Why a goal matters (目標が大切な理由)

QUESTION 1What makes a goal worth your time?どんな目標なら、時間をかける価値がありますか？

Follow up
  • What kind of change can it bring?
  • What might you leave until later?

What gets in the way (進めない理由)

QUESTION 2Which gets in the way more: time, money, or doubt?目標の邪魔になりやすいのは、時間、お金、自信のなさのどれですか？

Follow up
  • Why does it get in the way?
  • Which part can a person control?

What you dropped (あきらめたこと)

QUESTION 3When is dropping a goal a smart decision?目標をやめることが賢い判断になるのは、どんなときですか？

Follow up
  • What warning sign should someone notice?
  • Is stopping always the same as failing?

Who would notice (気づく人)

QUESTION 4How can sharing a goal make success feel different?目標を誰かに話すと、達成したときの気持ちはどう変わりますか？

Follow up
  • Who, if anyone, would understand the effort?
  • Would you share it with one person or keep it private?

A realistic limit (現実的な限界)

QUESTION 5What kind of goal is okay to change as life changes?生活が変わったとき、どんな目標なら変えてもよいと思いますか？

Follow up
  • What part should stay important?
  • Is it okay to make a goal smaller when life gets busy?

This week's step (今週の一歩)

QUESTION 6What's one useful first step toward a difficult goal?難しい目標に向かう、役に立つ最初の一歩は何ですか？

Follow up
  • When could someone do it?
  • What would make it easier?

Today's feedback (今日のフィードバック)

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
