Kindness when the food is not for you (好みでない料理への思いやり)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Food offered by another person can carry effort, hospitality, family history, and pride.人から出された料理には、手間、もてなしの気持ち、家族の歴史、誇りが込められていることがあります。hospitalityもてなし
That social meaning can make a simple statement of dislike feel unexpectedly harsh.その意味があるため、嫌いだと正直に言うだけでも強く聞こえることがあります。
Trying a small amount can show courtesy without requiring us to pretend that we love the dish.少し食べることで、大好きなふりをせずに礼儀を示せます。
A warm, specific comment about the effort or another part of the meal can remain completely sincere.手間や別の料理について具体的に温かく伝えることは、十分に誠実です。
Dietary needs, allergies, and strong physical reactions require clear boundaries rather than politeness through eating.食事上の制限、アレルギー、強い身体反応がある場合は、食べて礼儀を示すより明確に断る必要があります。
Our relationship with the host and the formality of the setting shape how directly we can respond.相手との関係や場の改まり方によって、どのくらい直接言えるかが変わります。
Thoughtful hosts often prefer useful information to praise or enthusiasm that is clearly insincere.思いやりのあるホストは、明らかに本心ではない褒め言葉や反応より、役立つ情報を好むことがあります。
A food that everyone else adores can make us question or hide our own reaction.ほかの人がみんな大好きな料理だと、自分の反応を疑ったり隠したりすることがあります。
Taste can evolve, although repeatedly eating something out of duty does not guarantee affection.好みは変わることがありますが、義務で何度も食べても好きになるとは限りません。
Good table manners make room for both the relationship and a person's genuine limits.よい食卓の礼儀は、人間関係も、その人の本当の限界も守ります。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Eating to be polite (礼儀のために食べるもの)

WARM-UP 1When might someone eat a food just to be polite?礼儀のために、食べ物を食べることがあるのはどんなときですか？

Follow up
  • Which situation creates the most pressure?
  • When is it still reasonable to refuse?

An awkward table (困る食卓)

WARM-UP 2Which is harder to refuse: a friend's homemade dish or an expensive restaurant dish?友人の手料理とレストランの高価な料理では、どちらが断りにくいですか？

Follow up
  • Why does that one create pressure?
  • When would refusal be easier?

The food (その食べ物)

QUESTION 1What taste or texture is hardest to hide your reaction to?反応を隠すのがいちばん難しい味や食感は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why does the reaction happen so quickly?
  • How could a host notice without words?

The hardest place to refuse (断りにくい場所)

QUESTION 2Where is it hardest to refuse food: someone's home, work, or a restaurant?食べ物を断りにくいのは、誰かの家、職場、レストランのどこですか？

Follow up
  • What kind of pressure appears there?
  • When is refusing still reasonable?

Would anyone notice? (気づかれる？)

QUESTION 3How can a host tell that a guest doesn't like a dish?客が料理を好きではないと、ホストはどうすれば分かりますか？

Follow up
  • What might the host misunderstand?
  • How can they make refusal feel safe?

What you would say (やさしく伝える)

QUESTION 4How would you kindly say that you didn't like a dish?料理が好みではなかったと、どのように優しく伝えますか？

Follow up
  • How can you be honest without judging the cooking?
  • What useful detail could you add?

Everyone else loves it (みんなが好きなもの)

QUESTION 5Why do some popular foods divide opinion?人気があっても好き嫌いが分かれる食べ物があるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • Is taste, texture, or expectation usually the reason?
  • Which food is a good example?

Honesty or feelings (正直さか気持ちか)

QUESTION 6Which matters more at dinner: honesty or protecting the host's feelings?食事の場では、正直さとホストの気持ちを守ることのどちらが大切ですか？

Follow up
  • What harm can polite dishonesty cause?
  • When does being polite become dishonest?
