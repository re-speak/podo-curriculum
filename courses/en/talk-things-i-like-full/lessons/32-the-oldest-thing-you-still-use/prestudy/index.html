What makes an object last (物が長く使える理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
An old object survives not only through durability, but because someone repeatedly chooses to use it.古い物が残るのは、丈夫だからだけでなく、誰かが繰り返し使うことを選ぶからです。
Durable materials and straightforward construction can make maintenance and repair possible.丈夫な材料と単純な作りは、手入れや修理をしやすくします。maintenance手入れ
Familiarity matters because we understand the object's habits, limits, and small imperfections.使い慣れた物は、その特徴や限界、小さな欠点まで分かっています。
Replacing it may impose the cost of learning a new system for only a minor practical gain.買い替えると、わずかな利点のために新しい使い方を覚える必要があるかもしれません。
An older tool may be less efficient on paper yet more dependable for a familiar task.古い道具は数字上は効率が低くても、慣れた作業では頼りになることがあります。
Repairs form a visible record of how the object and its owner have adapted over time.修理の跡は、物と持ち主が時間とともにどう対応してきたかを残します。
Keeping every old object is different from deliberately keeping one that still serves a purpose.古い物を何でも残すことと、今も役立つ物を意識して残すことは違います。
Some products are built to last, whereas others make repair difficult or uneconomical.長持ちするように作られた物もあれば、修理が難しい物や高くつく物もあります。
The oldest useful object in a home can reveal how technology and daily habits have changed around it.家で今も使ういちばん古い物から、技術や日々の習慣の変化が見えます。
What we refuse to replace can reveal which qualities newer products have failed to improve.買い替えたくない物は、新しい製品でも改善できていない大切な性質を教えてくれます。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Your oldest useful thing (いちばん古い実用品)

WARM-UP 1What's the oldest thing you still use?今も使っているいちばん古い物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Where do you keep it?
  • What does it look like now?

Used recently (最近使った古い物)

WARM-UP 2Which old item did you use most recently?最近使った古い物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • What made it useful?
  • How often does it still earn a place in your day?

How old (古さ)

QUESTION 1How old is the object?その物はどのくらい古いですか？

Follow up
  • How certain are you about its age?
  • What else has changed since you got it?

How you got it (手に入れた方法)

QUESTION 2How did you get it?それをどうやって手に入れましたか？

Follow up
  • Was it new at the time?
  • Who chose it?

Why you kept it (残した理由)

QUESTION 3Why have you kept using it?なぜそれを使い続けていますか？

Follow up
  • Which quality matters most?
  • Has it ever needed repair?

Keeper or thrower (残す人？捨てる人？)

QUESTION 4Are you generally a keeper or a thrower?普段、物を残すほうですか、捨てるほうですか？

Follow up
  • What is easiest for you to discard?
  • Which category is hardest?

Repair or replace (修理か買い替えか)

QUESTION 5When do you repair something instead of replacing it?どんなとき、買い替えずに修理しますか？

Follow up
  • What makes a repair worth the effort?
  • When is replacement the better choice?

Never get rid of (手放さない物)

QUESTION 6What will you never get rid of?絶対に手放さない物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Is its value practical or personal?
  • Who might use it after you?
