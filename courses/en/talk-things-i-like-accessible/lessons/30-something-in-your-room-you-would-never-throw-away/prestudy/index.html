Something in your room you would never throw away (部屋にある絶対に捨てないもの)

Let's tell the story of an object you would keep. Please read the title aloud.手元に残したい物の物語を話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

Why ordinary objects become irreplaceable (普通の物がかけがえなくなる理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
An ordinary object can become valuable because of the story attached to it.普通の物でも、そこに結びついた物語によって、価値のあるものになります。
Its market price may have little connection to its sentimental value.市場での値段と、その物への思い入れは、ほとんど関係がないかもしれません。sentimental value思い入れ
A gift can preserve a relationship even when the giver is far away.贈り物は、贈った人が遠くにいても、つながりを残してくれます。
Marks and damage can become part of the memory instead of reducing the value.傷や損傷が、価値を下げるのではなく思い出の一部になることがあります。
Keeping everything makes it harder to see which objects truly matter.何でも取っておくと、本当に大切な物が見えにくくなります。
A keepsake earns its space by helping us remember something specific.思い出の品は、何か具体的なことを思い出させるから、置いておく価値があります。keepsake思い出の品
Other people may not understand why the object matters.ほかの人には、なぜその物が大切なのか分からないことがあります。
Throwing something away can feel like choosing which memories remain visible.物を捨てることは、どの思い出を目に見える形で残すか選ぶように感じられます。
A photograph can preserve the appearance, but not always the physical feeling.写真は見た目を残せますが、実物の感触までは残せないことがあります。
The objects we still keep in ten years may tell a different story about us.10年後にも残っている物は、自分について別の物語を伝えるかもしれません。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

The object (その物)

WARM-UP 1What object would you never throw away?絶対に捨てたくない物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • What makes it irreplaceable?
  • What would you notice first if it disappeared?

Your oldest object (いちばん古い持ち物)

WARM-UP 2Which object you own has been with you the longest?持っている物の中で、いちばん長く手元にあるものは何ですか？

Follow up
  • How often do you use or notice it?
  • What shows its age?

How you got it (手に入れた経緯)

QUESTION 1How did you get the object?その物をどうやって手に入れましたか？

Follow up
  • What was happening in your life then?
  • What makes the way you got it memorable?

How long (持っている期間)

QUESTION 2How long have you had it?どのくらい長く持っていますか？

Follow up
  • How has it changed over that time?
  • What part has stayed the same?

Who understands (大切さが分かる人)

QUESTION 3Who would best understand why the object matters to you?その物が自分にとって大切な理由を、いちばん理解してくれそうなのは誰ですか？

Follow up
  • How would that person explain its value?
  • What would be hardest to explain to a stranger?

Possible regret (捨てたら後悔する物)

QUESTION 4What kind of object are you most likely to regret throwing away?どんな物を捨てると、いちばん後悔しそうですか？

Follow up
  • What memory could make it hard to replace?
  • How do you decide when to let something go?

Ten years from now (10年後)

QUESTION 5Which object will you still have in ten years?10年後にも持っていそうな物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • What might happen to it by then?
  • Who might care about it after you?

The story to share (伝えたい物語)

QUESTION 6Tell me the story of the object you would show me first.私に最初に見せたい物の物語を話してください。

Follow up
  • Which detail should I notice?
  • What would a photograph fail to capture?

Today's feedback (今日のフィードバック)

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
