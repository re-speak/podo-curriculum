Why some advice stays (助言が心に残る理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Advice often endures because of its timing, not merely its wording.助言は言葉そのものだけでなく、聞いたタイミングによって残ります。
An ordinary sentence can acquire unusual force during a difficult decision.難しい決断の中では、普通の一言が特別な力を持つことがあります。
The source matters when we trust the speaker's experience, judgment, or care.相手の経験、判断、思いやりを信頼していると、その言葉は大切になります。
We may understand the literal message immediately while taking years to grasp its value.表面の意味はすぐ分かっても、その価値を理解するまで何年もかかることがあります。
Durable advice usually leaves room for context rather than pretending to be universal.長く役立つ助言は、すべてに当てはまるふりをせず、状況に合わせる余地があります。
Poor advice can sound decisive while ignoring cost, risk, power, or personality.悪い助言は断定的に聞こえても、費用、危険、力関係、性格を無視することがあります。
Advice can be sound even when the listener is not ready or the moment is wrong.聞く人に準備がない、または時期が違えば、よい助言でも従えないことがあります。
Following advice does not by itself prove that the advice produced the outcome.助言に従ったことだけでは、その助言が結果を生んだとは証明できません。
Passing advice on inevitably carries our own interpretation and omissions.助言を人に伝えるとき、自分の解釈や省いた部分も必ず一緒に伝わります。
A useful test is whether the advice clarifies a choice without taking ownership of it away.よい助言かどうかは、相手から決定権を奪わずに選択を明確にできるかで分かります。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Why it stays (心に残る理由)

WARM-UP 1Why does certain advice remain memorable long after it is first heard?最初に聞いてから長い時間がたっても、心に残る助言があるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • How strongly does the moment of hearing it matter?
  • Why might a simple principle outlast a more complex message?

Memorable wording (記憶に残る言葉)

WARM-UP 2Which qualities make the wording of advice especially memorable?助言の言い方を特に記憶に残りやすくするのは、どのような特徴ですか？

Follow up
  • How much does brevity contribute?
  • Why can an image or concrete example strengthen the message?

Who says it (言う人)

QUESTION 1Why can the source of advice matter as much as the words themselves?助言では、言葉そのものと同じくらい、誰が言ったかが重要になるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • What gives the speaker credibility?
  • How might identical advice feel different from another source?

The right moment (意味を持つ時)

QUESTION 2What makes advice suddenly relevant at a particular moment in someone's life?ある瞬間に、助言が急に自分に関係するように感じられるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • Can valuable advice be given before someone is ready for it?
  • Which change can make an old message newly visible?

Understanding later (あとで分かること)

QUESTION 3Why can experience make old advice clearer in hindsight?経験を重ねると、昔の助言が振り返って分かりやすくなるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • Which kind of experience can change its interpretation?
  • Can someone understand advice without ultimately agreeing with it?

When it helps (役立つ時)

QUESTION 4Which conditions make advice useful, and which conditions expose its limits?どのような条件で助言は役立ち、どのような条件で限界が見えますか？

Follow up
  • How strongly does context determine its usefulness?
  • When should the underlying idea be adapted rather than followed literally?

Worth sharing (伝える価値)

QUESTION 5What makes a piece of advice valuable enough to pass on?ある助言を人に伝える価値があると判断するのは、どのようなときですか？

Follow up
  • Which part of the person's situation should be understood first?
  • How can advice be offered without becoming an instruction?

Advice ignored (助言に従わない理由)

QUESTION 6Why might people reject advice that appears sensible?もっともらしい助言でも、人が受け入れないことがあるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • How can timing weaken an otherwise sound message?
  • When can a different choice be reasonable despite the advice?
