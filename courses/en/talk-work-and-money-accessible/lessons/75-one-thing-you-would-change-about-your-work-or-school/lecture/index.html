WORK AND MONEY

One thing you would change about your work or school (仕事や学校で変えたい一つのこと)

Let's describe a problem and propose a realistic change. Please read the title aloud.問題を説明し、現実的な変更を提案しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

A useful change starts with a clear problem (役立つ変更は明確な問題から始まる)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Places where people work or study often contain problems that have become normal.働いたり学んだりする場所では、問題が当たり前になっていることがあります。
A specific issue is easier to change than a broad complaint.広い不満より、具体的な問題のほうが変えやすくなります。
The choice to raise an issue can carry different risks for different people.問題を提起するリスクは、人によって違います。raise an issue問題を提起する
A good proposal explains who benefits and who carries the cost.よい提案は、誰が利益を得て、誰が負担するかを説明します。
The person closest to a problem may not control the solution.問題に最も近い人が、解決を決められるとは限りません。
For a low-risk proposal, a small trial can test a change before it spreads.リスクの低い提案なら、小さく試すことで、広げる前に変更を検証できます。
Some concerns need a confidential or formal route.内容によっては、秘密を守る正式な経路が必要です。
A proposal can be useful even when it is revised, delayed, or rejected.提案は、修正されたり、延期されたり、却下されたりしても役立つことがあります。
Opposition may reveal a real constraint.反対意見から、実際の制約が見えることがあります。
A respectful response separates the issue from the person.丁寧な応答は、問題と人を分けて考えます。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

One change (一つの変更)

WARM-UP 1Which change would most improve a place where people work or study?人が働いたり学んだりする場所を最も改善する変更は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Which daily problem would disappear first?
  • Would students, workers, or managers notice first?

The problem nobody fixes (誰も直さない問題)

WARM-UP 2What's a problem at work or school that everyone notices but nobody fixes?職場や学校で、みんな気づいているのに誰も直さない問題は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why does it remain?
  • If none comes to mind, would noise, meetings, or scheduling be a likely example?

Who might object (反対する人)

QUESTION 1Why might someone oppose a useful change at work or school?職場や学校で役立つ変化に、誰かが反対するのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • Would cost, fairness, or extra work worry them most?
  • What evidence could reduce that worry?

A new problem (新しく生まれる問題)

QUESTION 2What new problem could a useful change create?役立つ変更でも、どんな新しい問題を生む可能性がありますか？

Follow up
  • Who might be inconvenienced?
  • Could a small test reveal the problem?

Whether it was raised (提起したか)

QUESTION 3What's a safe way to raise a problem at work or school?不必要なリスクを最も抑えて、職場や学校の問題を提起できるのはどの経路ですか？

Follow up
  • Would a direct talk, a private talk, or a report be safest?
  • What protection would make speaking up easier?

A fair response (公平な対応)

QUESTION 4After someone reports a problem, what response would feel fair?誰かが問題を伝えた後、どんな対応なら公平だと感じますか？

Follow up
  • How quickly should someone reply?
  • What clear next step should they give?

The safest next step (安全な次の一歩)

QUESTION 5When is a small test better than making a big change at once?大きな変化を一度に行うより、小さく試すほうがよいのはどんなときですか？

Follow up
  • Which result would prove the test helped?
  • What warning sign should end the test?

Saying no well (上手な断り方)

QUESTION 6How can you say no to a suggestion without shutting the person down?相手の意見を封じずに、提案を断るにはどうすればよいですか？

Follow up
  • What useful part could you recognize?
  • What alternative could you offer?

Today's feedback (今日のフィードバック)

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
