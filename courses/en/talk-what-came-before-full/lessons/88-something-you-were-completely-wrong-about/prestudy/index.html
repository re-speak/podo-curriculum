How certainty survives bad information (誤った情報でも確信が続く理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article.
A misconception becomes convincing when it fits a coherent story we already accept.誤解は、すでに受け入れている筋の通った話に合うと説得力を持ちます。
Repeated exposure increases familiarity, which can be mistaken for evidence.繰り返し触れると親しみが増し、それを根拠と間違えることがあります。
Source memory often fades while the claim itself remains available.情報源の記憶が薄れても、主張そのものは残ることがあります。
Trust can carry inaccurate information even when nobody intends to mislead.誰もだますつもりがなくても、信頼によって不正確な情報が広がります。
Correction can feel unstable because it disrupts a familiar explanation.訂正は慣れた説明を崩すため、最初は不安定に感じられます。
The threat of embarrassment can turn a factual correction into a defense of identity.恥をかく不安によって、事実の訂正が自分らしさを守る行動に変わることがあります。
Effective correction combines verifiable evidence with a face-saving path to revision.効果的な訂正には、確認できる根拠と、面目を保って考え直す道があります。verifiable確認できる
Discovering one error should encourage better calibration, not certainty that every other claim is false.一つの誤りを知ったら、ほかの主張もすべて誤りだと決めつけず、確信の度合いをより適切に調整すべきです。
Confidence becomes more reliable when it includes source, evidence, and uncertainty.情報源、根拠、不確かさを含めると、確信はより信頼できるものになります。
Revising a belief in response to evidence is intellectual flexibility, not failure.根拠に応じて考えを変えることは、失敗ではなく知的な柔軟さです。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of the conversation.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。会話の中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Use these pages as a flexible pool. Ask one question, react naturally, and share a brief real answer of your own when it adds to the exchange. Skip freely and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Completely wrong (完全な思い違い)

WARM-UP 1Which once-common belief was overturned most convincingly by later evidence?かつて広く信じられていた考えのうち、後の証拠で最も明確に覆されたものは何ですか？

Follow up
  • Which example best shows misplaced certainty?
  • What made the claim persuasive at the time?

The old idea (以前の考え)

WARM-UP 2What precise claim made up the misconception?どんな具体的な主張が、その誤解を作っていましたか？

Follow up
  • Was the belief specific?
  • What did it explain?

How long (信じた期間)

QUESTION 1For how long, or how broadly, did the misconception remain convincing?その誤解はどのくらいの期間、またはどのくらい広く信じられていましたか？

Follow up
  • Did confidence grow?
  • Was it ever questioned?

Where it came from (情報源)

QUESTION 2Which source, repetition, or assumption made the belief credible?どんな情報源、繰り返し、前提が、その考えを信じさせましたか？

Follow up
  • Was the source trusted?
  • Was the original source remembered?

Finding out (間違いに気づく)

QUESTION 3Which evidence or event overturned the misconception?どんな根拠や出来事が、その誤解を覆しましたか？

Follow up
  • What evidence mattered?
  • Was the correction immediate?

The first reaction (最初の反応)

QUESTION 4Did the person revise the belief immediately, resist, or reconsider gradually?その人はすぐに考えを変えましたか、抵抗しましたか、それとも徐々に考え直しましたか？

Follow up
  • What supported acceptance, resistance, or delay?
  • Was embarrassment involved, or was another concern more important?

What changed after (その後の変化)

QUESTION 5How did the correction change later judgment or behaviour?その訂正は、その後の判断や行動をどう変えましたか？

Follow up
  • Which information habit changed most?
  • What lasting effect did the revision have?

What are you sure about? (今確かなこと)

QUESTION 6Which current belief feels well supported, and what evidence could still revise it?今よく根拠があると思う考えは何で、どんな証拠ならさらに見直しますか？

Follow up
  • How strong is the evidence?
  • What could change your mind?
