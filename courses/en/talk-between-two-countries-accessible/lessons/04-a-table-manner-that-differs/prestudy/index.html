BETWEEN TWO COUNTRIES

A table manner that differs between countries (国によって違う食事のマナー)

Let's compare one table habit and the meaning people give it. Please read the title aloud.食卓での習慣を一つ比べ、その意味について話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

The hidden rules at a table (食卓にある見えないルール)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Table manners feel natural because we learn them when we are young.食事のマナーは説明できるようになるずっと前から身につけるため、自然に感じられます。
So one small action can mean different things in two countries.そのため、小さな動作が二つの国でまったく違う意味を持つことがあります。
Picking up a bowl may be polite in one place and unusual in another.お椀を持つことが、ある場所では礼儀正しく、別の場所では珍しく見えることがあります。
There may also be quiet rules about sharing food or starting the meal.食べる順番、料理の分け方、食べ始める時にも、暗黙のルールがあります。
Visitors often notice a rule only after someone reacts.旅行者は、誰かに反応されて初めてそのルールに気づくことがよくあります。
Feeling embarrassed makes the moment easy to remember, but it may not explain the rule.恥ずかしさでその瞬間は記憶に残りますが、理由まで分かるとは限りません。
Asking who taught the habit can show differences between families, ages, and regions.その習慣を誰に教わったか聞くと、家庭、世代、地域の違いが見えてきます。
Some rules are still important, while others are becoming more flexible.今も大切なルールもあれば、柔軟になっているものもあります。flexible柔軟な
Knowing why a rule exists makes it easier to adjust.ルールを知ることは役立ちますが、目的まで分かると合わせやすくなります。
A respectful guest watches, asks, and does not treat one table as a whole country.礼儀ある客は、よく見て、質問し、一つの食卓を国全体だと思わないようにします。respectful礼儀をわきまえた

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Start here (ここから)

WARM-UP 1Do you pick your bowl up when you eat?食べるとき、お椀を手に持ちますか？

Follow up
  • When did you learn that habit?
  • Does everyone in your home do the same?

A confusing moment (迷った場面)

WARM-UP 2What might make a guest unsure what to do at someone else's table?ほかの人の食卓で、客がどうすればよいか迷うのはどんなときですか？

Follow up
  • Which quiet rule could cause hesitation?
  • How could the guest decide what to do?

At your table (あなたの食卓)

QUESTION 1What other table habit is fixed in your home?あなたの家で決まっている食卓の習慣はほかに何ですか？

Follow up
  • When do you notice someone doing it differently?
  • Does the rule change for guests?

Who starts? (誰が始める？)

QUESTION 2Who usually starts eating first?ふつう誰が最初に食べ始めますか？

Follow up
  • What happens if someone starts early?
  • Is the rule different at a restaurant?

What feels rude? (失礼に感じること)

QUESTION 3What action at the table feels rude to you?食卓で何をされると失礼だと感じますか？

Follow up
  • Why does that action bother people?
  • Would you correct a visitor?

Across countries (国をまたぐと)

QUESTION 4Which table habit changes most between countries you know?知っている国の中で、いちばん違いが大きい食卓の習慣は何ですか？

Follow up
  • How could a visitor discover that rule?
  • Would you like to hear what I do differently?

Who taught you? (誰に教わった？)

QUESTION 5Who taught you your table manners?食事のマナーは誰に教わりましたか？

Follow up
  • Which rule did they care about most?
  • Have you kept all of their rules?

Still important? (今も大切？)

QUESTION 6Which table rules do people care less about now?今はあまり気にされなくなった食事のルールは何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why has that rule weakened?
  • Which rule should not disappear?

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
