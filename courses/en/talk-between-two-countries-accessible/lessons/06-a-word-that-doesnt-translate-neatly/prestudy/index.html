Let's explore a word whose meaning or feeling changes in translation. Please read the title aloud.訳すと意味や感覚が変わる言葉について話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

What gets lost between languages (言語の間で失われるもの)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
A dictionary can give a similar word without giving the same feeling.辞書は対応する言葉を示せても、同じ感覚までは伝えられないことがあります。
Meaning also changes with the speaker, relationship, and situation.意味は、話す人、相手との関係、状況にも左右されます。relationship関係
So one Japanese word may need a full English sentence.そのため、日本語の一語が、場面によっては英語の一文を必要とすることがあります。
The closest translation may be correct but sound warmer, colder, stronger, or more formal.いちばん近い訳でも、より温かく、冷たく、強く、または堅く聞こえることがあります。formalかたい
People often explain the situation around the word to fill the gap.話し手は、その言葉が使われる状況を説明して差を埋めることがよくあります。
Examples show when the word sounds natural and when it sounds strange.例があると、その言葉が自然な時と、不自然に感じる時が分かります。
Tone, timing, and shared knowledge can carry as much information as the word.口調、タイミング、共有している知識は、言葉そのものと同じくらい情報を伝えます。
That is why a correct subtitle can still feel wrong.そのため、技術的には正しい字幕でも、違和感があることがあります。
These gaps help us notice hidden ideas in our own language.こうした違いを学ぶと、自分の言語に含まれる前提にも気づけます。
Translation means choosing what the listener most needs, not only replacing words.翻訳は単語を置き換えるだけでなく、聞き手に最も必要なものを選ぶことです。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Start here (ここから)

WARM-UP 1Which English word would you like to understand better?もっとよく理解したい英単語は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Where have you seen or heard it?
  • Do new words stick better from songs, shows, or conversations?

Hard to explain (説明しにくい言葉)

WARM-UP 2Which is harder to translate: a joke, a feeling, or a polite phrase?冗談、気持ち、丁寧な表現の中で、いちばん訳しにくいのはどれですか？

Follow up
  • Why is that one difficult?
  • Which is easier to translate: facts or feelings?

A new word (新しい言葉)

QUESTION 1What's one English word that sounds strange in Japanese?日本語にすると不思議に聞こえる英単語は何ですか？

Follow up
  • What is the usual translation?
  • Do English words sound stranger in ads or everyday conversation?

What it really means (本当の意味)

QUESTION 2Which Japanese word needs a full English sentence to explain?英語では一文使わないと説明しにくい日本語は何ですか？

Follow up
  • When do people use it?
  • Which is harder to explain in English: a food name or a feeling?

Dictionary trouble (辞書の訳の問題)

QUESTION 3When does a dictionary translation sound wrong?辞書の訳が不自然に聞こえるのは、どんなときですか？

Follow up
  • Can the word still be correct?
  • What context changes it?

Who you say it to (誰に言う？)

QUESTION 4Which word or phrase changes meaning most depending on who you're talking to?話す相手によって意味が大きく変わる言葉や表現は何ですか？

Follow up
  • How does it change with a stranger?
  • Do you speak differently to a friend and a boss?

A funny mistake (笑ってしまう翻訳ミス)

QUESTION 5What kind of translation mistake can make a serious scene funny?どんな翻訳ミスで、真面目な場面が面白くなってしまいますか？

Follow up
  • What meaning was probably intended?
  • Have subtitles ever made you laugh?

Meaning or feeling? (意味か感覚か)

QUESTION 6Which matters more in translation: exact meaning or the same feeling?翻訳では、正確な意味と同じ感覚のどちらが大切ですか？

Follow up
  • When does your choice matter most?
  • When would the other one win?
