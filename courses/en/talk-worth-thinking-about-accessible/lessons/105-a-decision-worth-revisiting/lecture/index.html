Cancelling without damaging trust (信頼を損なわずに予定をキャンセルする)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article.
At four in the afternoon, Leo realizes he is too sick to attend a friend's birthday dinner.午後四時、レオは体調が悪く、友人の誕生日の夕食会に参加できないと気づきます。
The reservation is at seven, and his friend has already paid for a group activity.予約は七時で、友人はすでにグループ活動の代金を払っています。
Cancelling is sometimes necessary, but the timing and the message affect how it feels.キャンセルが必要なこともありますが、伝える時期や内容によって受け取られ方が変わります。
Illness, dangerous weather, or a family emergency usually feels different from getting a better invitation.病気や危険な天候、家族の緊急事態は、もっと魅力的な誘いを受けた場合とは違って感じられます。
The fairest notice is often as soon as the person knows they cannot come.行けないと分かった時点ですぐ知らせることが、最も公平な連絡になりやすいです。
A text may be enough for casual plans, while an important event or a serious cost may need a call.気軽な予定ならメッセージで十分でも、大切な行事や大きな費用が関わる場合は電話が必要かもしれません。
If money for a ticket or meal cannot be returned, offering to cover the cost can show responsibility.チケットや食事代が返金されない場合、費用を負担すると申し出ることで責任を示せます。
Being extremely tired is less clear because rest can be a real need, but the other person may also have made an effort.疲れ切っている場合は判断が難しく、休息が本当に必要な一方で、相手も準備をしているかもしれません。
An honest apology explains enough, avoids excuses, and offers a practical next step.誠実な謝罪は、必要な説明をし、言い訳を避け、具体的な次の行動を示します。
One cancellation rarely ends a friendship, but cancelling again and again without making things right can slowly change trust.一度のキャンセルで友情が終わることは少なくても、埋め合わせのないキャンセルが続けば、信頼は少しずつ変わります。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of the conversation.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。会話の中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Use these pages as a flexible pool. Ask one question, react naturally, and share a brief real answer or relevant perspective of your own when it adds to the exchange. Skip freely and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Follow up

  • Which reasons would not feel fair?

Fair notice (公平な連絡時期)

WARM-UP 2How much notice feels fair when someone cancels?キャンセルするとき、どのくらい前に知らせるのが公平ですか？

Follow up
  • Does the type of plan matter?
  • What if they only know an hour before?

A text or a call (メッセージか電話か)

QUESTION 1Is a text message enough, or should the person call?メッセージだけで十分ですか、それとも電話するべきですか？

Follow up
  • When would a call matter?
  • Which would you prefer to receive?

The cost of cancelling (キャンセルで生じる費用)

QUESTION 2When should someone offer to pay for a cost caused by cancelling?キャンセルで費用が発生した場合、どんなときに支払いを申し出るべきですか？

Follow up
  • What if a ticket cannot be refunded?
  • Should friends split the cost?

Too many cancellations (多すぎるキャンセル)

QUESTION 3How many cancellations would make you stop inviting someone?何回キャンセルされたら、その人を誘わなくなりますか？

Follow up
  • Does the reason matter?
  • What could rebuild trust?

Too exhausted (疲れ切っているとき)

QUESTION 4Can being exhausted be a good enough reason to cancel?疲れ切っていることは、キャンセルする十分な理由になりますか？

Follow up
  • Does it depend on the plan?
  • What could they suggest instead?

Follow up
