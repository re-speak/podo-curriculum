Three things you would take to a desert island (無人島に持っていく三つの物)

Let's choose what would matter most on a desert island. Please read the title aloud.無人島で最も大切になるものを選びましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

What makes an item essential (物を必需品にするもの)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
A desert-island list compresses survival, comfort, and identity into only three objects.無人島のリストでは、生存、快適さ、自分らしさを三つの物に絞り込みます。
An essential item addresses a need whose delay would carry a serious cost.必需品とは、先延ばしにすると重大な結果が出る必要を満たす物です。
Practical equipment can outrank a treasured possession when conditions are harsh.厳しい状況では、大切な持ち物より実用的な道具が優先されることがあります。
Comfort is not automatically frivolous; rest and hope can support endurance.快適さは必ずしも無駄ではなく、休息や希望が耐える力を支えます。
A versatile object earns its place by meeting several different needs.多用途の物は、複数の必要を満たすことで選ぶ価値が高まります。versatile多用途の
The object that almost made the list reveals which need was sacrificed.最後に外した物は、どの必要を犠牲にしたかを示します。
An apparently useless object may protect memory, motivation, or a sense of self.一見役に立たない物でも、記憶や意欲、自分らしさを守る場合があります。
Regret often attaches to the meaningful object left behind rather than the practical object selected.後悔は、選んだ実用品より、置いてきた大切な物に結びつくことがあります。
Adding a companion changes the scenario from inventory management to shared responsibility.同行者が加わると、持ち物の管理ではなく、責任を分け合う状況に変わります。
No list is sensible until the environment and available resources are known.環境や利用できる資源が分からなければ、妥当なリストは作れません。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

The hardest first-day problem (最初の日に最も大変なこと)

WARM-UP 1Which problem would be hardest to face on your first day on a desert island?無人島での最初の一日に、どの問題に向き合うのが最も大変ですか？

Follow up
  • Would water, shelter, or being alone worry you most?
  • What could make the first day easier?

Three things (持っていく三つ)

WARM-UP 2If you could take only three things, what would you choose?三つだけ持って行けるなら、何を選びますか？

Follow up
  • Which one would help you survive first?
  • Which one would make you feel less alone?

One useful object (役立つ一つの物)

QUESTION 1What ordinary object could solve two different problems on the island?島で二つの問題を解決できそうな普通の物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Would a pot, rope, or mirror work?
  • What two problems would it solve?

One comfort item (心を楽にする物一つ)

QUESTION 2Would one comfort item be worth taking to a desert island?無人島に、気持ちを楽にする物を一つ持って行く価値はありますか？

Follow up
  • Would you choose music, a book, or a favourite snack?
  • If you took none, what practical item would replace it?

After the first night (最初の夜のあと)

QUESTION 3What would you regret not bringing after the first night?最初の夜の後、持ってこなかったことを後悔しそうな物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Would you miss a light, blanket, or tool most?
  • What could replace it?

A cold island (寒い島)

QUESTION 4Would you choose different island supplies in cold weather?寒い天気の島なら、違う道具を選びますか？

Follow up
  • Would warm clothing or a fire tool come first?
  • Which tropical item would be less useful?

A companion or an object (同行者か物か)

QUESTION 5What could one good companion provide that no object could?よい仲間一人は、どんな物にもできない何を与えてくれますか？

Follow up
  • What would you share responsibility for?
  • What would be easier alone?

One regret (後悔する一つ)

QUESTION 6After a week, which of your three choices might you regret?一週間後、選んだ三つのうち、どれを後悔しそうですか？

Follow up
  • What would you wish you had brought instead?
  • Which choice would still feel right?
