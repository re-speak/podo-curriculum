Safety before possessions (持ち物より安全)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
The scenario begins only after every person and pet is safely outside.この想像は、人もペットも全員安全に外へ出たあとから始まります。
No possession justifies re-entering smoke or fire; emergency instructions take priority.持ち物のために煙や火の中へ戻るべきではなく、緊急時の指示が最優先です。
Immediate evacuation takes priority over retrieving even an irreplaceable possession.かけがえのない持ち物であっても、取りに行くことより、すぐ避難することが優先です。
Only an object already on the safe exit route belongs in the thought experiment.安全な避難経路にすでにある物だけが、この思考実験の対象です。
An irreplaceable object carries information, history, or meaning that no new copy can restore.irreplaceable な物には、新しい物では戻せない情報、歴史、意味があります。
The most expensive possession may matter less than a cheap object connected to memory or identity.最も高価な持ち物より、記憶や自分らしさと結びついた安い物のほうが大切な場合があります。
Backups can protect photographs and documents, although the original object may still carry meaning.バックアップで写真や書類を守れますが、元の物に意味が残る場合もあります。
A choice that looks strange from outside may be coherent once its personal history is known.外から不思議に見える選択でも、その個人的な歴史を知れば筋が通る場合があります。
Losing something before may increase care, or may teach that memory can survive without the object.以前何かを失った経験から、より大切に守ることも、物がなくても記憶は残ると学ぶこともあります。
Backups, inventories, alarms, and evacuation plans matter more than deciding what to retrieve during danger.危険なときに何を取りに行くかより、バックアップ、一覧、警報、避難計画のほうが重要です。inventory一覧

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

One thing (一つの物)

WARM-UP 1Once everyone is safe, which possession would be hardest to lose?全員が安全になった後、失うのが最もつらい持ち物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Would you choose photos, a gift, or something you use every day?
  • Could a photo or copy protect part of it?

Why that one (それを選ぶ理由)

WARM-UP 2What makes one old object impossible to replace?一つの古い物が、代えられないものになるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • Do you still use it, or does it hold a memory?
  • Would a photo or copy keep what matters?

The memory (その物の思い出)

QUESTION 1What memory does that object bring back?その物を見ると、どんな思い出がよみがえりますか？

Follow up
  • Who is part of that memory?
  • When did you last look at or use it?

Photos, letters, or something practical (写真、手紙、実用品)

QUESTION 2Would you save photos, letters, or one practical item?写真、手紙、実用的な物一つのどれを持ち出しますか？

Follow up
  • Which of those would be hardest to replace?
  • What would you save instead of those three?

Can a digital copy be enough? (デジタルコピーで十分か)

QUESTION 3Can a digital copy preserve what matters about an old object?デジタルのコピーで、古い物の大切なところを残せますか？

Follow up
  • What can a copy keep?
  • What disappears without the original object?

Someone who understands (分かってくれる人)

QUESTION 4Who would understand why that object matters to you?その物がなぜ大切なのか、誰なら分かってくれそうですか？

Follow up
  • Would you need to explain it?
  • Would it mean the same thing to them?

Preparing safely (安全な準備)

QUESTION 5What important object could you back up or photograph now?今、バックアップしたり写真に撮ったりできる大切な物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Would photos, documents, or messages need a backup?
  • Where would you keep the backup?

Something easy to replace (買い替えやすい物)

QUESTION 6Now the opposite: what would be easiest to replace?では反対に、最も簡単に買い替えられる物は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why doesn’t it carry the same meaning?
  • Would you replace it straight away?
