How certainty survives bad information (誤った情報でも確信が続く理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article.
A misconception can feel true when it fits an existing story.誤解は、すでにある話に合うと本当のように感じられます。
Repetition can create confidence without creating evidence.繰り返し聞くと、根拠がなくても自信が生まれます。
People may forget where the original claim came from.元の話をどこで聞いたか忘れることもあります。
A trusted person can pass on a mistake without intending harm.信頼する人が、悪気なく間違いを伝えることがあります。
Correct information may first feel less believable than the old idea.正しい情報が、最初は昔の考えより信じにくいこともあります。
Embarrassment can make someone defend the mistake longer.恥ずかしさのため、間違いを長く守ってしまうことがあります。
A good correction provides evidence and room to change calmly.よい訂正には、根拠と落ち着いて変わる余地があります。
Finding one mistake should lead to better checking, not certainty that everything else is wrong.一つの間違いを見つけたら、ほかもすべて間違いだと決めつけず、よりよく確かめるべきです。
People can learn to state how sure they are and why.どのくらい確かで、なぜそう思うかを言えるようになります。
Changing a belief is a strength when evidence changes.根拠が変わったときに考えを変えるのは強さです。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of the conversation.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。会話の中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Use these pages as a flexible pool. Ask one question, react naturally, and share a brief real answer of your own when it adds to the exchange. Skip freely and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

A surprising old belief (驚くような昔の考え)

WARM-UP 1What's something people used to believe that surprises you now?昔の人が信じていたことで、今のあなたが驚くものは何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why did it sound true?
  • What did people once believe about health or science?

A harmless wrong fact (害のない思い違い)

WARM-UP 2Which small misconception is surprisingly hard to correct?意外に訂正しにくい小さな思い違いは何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why does the wrong answer sound right?
  • Would a myth about food or animals be a good example?

Why wrong beliefs last (間違った考えが残る理由)

QUESTION 1What lets a wrong belief survive even when better information is available?よりよい情報があっても、間違った考えが残るのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • Why do people keep repeating it?
  • What might finally weaken it?

Where wrong ideas begin (間違った考えの始まり)

QUESTION 2Why can the source of a wrong idea still seem trustworthy?間違った考えの情報源でも、信頼できそうに見えるのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • Which sources seem believable?
  • How can someone check the original claim?

Evidence that changes a mind (考えを変える証拠)

QUESTION 3What makes a correction easier to accept?訂正を受け入れやすくするものは何ですか？

Follow up
  • Would a clear example or a trusted source help more?
  • When might a person still refuse to change their mind?

Admitting a mistake (間違いを認めること)

QUESTION 4How can embarrassment keep a person defending an idea they doubt?恥ずかしさによって、疑っている考えを守り続けてしまうのはなぜですか？

Follow up
  • What makes admitting it feel personal?
  • How can someone correct the idea without embarrassing the person?

A better judge afterward (後で判断がよくなること)

QUESTION 5What checking habit can someone learn from being confidently wrong?自信を持って間違えた経験から、どんな確認の習慣を学べますか？

Follow up
  • Would checking a second source help?
  • When is that habit most useful?

A belief you hold lightly (変わるかもしれない考え)

QUESTION 6How confident should you sound when you are not sure a claim is true?ある主張が本当か確信がないとき、どのくらい自信を持って話すべきですか？

Follow up
  • Would you say 'maybe' or give a percentage?
  • How certain are you about tomorrow's weather?
