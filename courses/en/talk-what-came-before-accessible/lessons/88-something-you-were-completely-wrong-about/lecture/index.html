How certainty survives bad information (誤った情報でも確信が続く理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article.
A misconception can feel true when it fits an existing story.誤解は、すでにある話に合うと本当のように感じられます。
Repetition can create confidence without creating evidence.繰り返し聞くと、根拠がなくても自信が生まれます。
People may forget where the original claim came from.元の話をどこで聞いたか忘れることもあります。
A trusted person can pass on a mistake without intending harm.信頼する人が、悪気なく間違いを伝えることがあります。
Correct information may first feel less believable than the old idea.正しい情報が、最初は昔の考えより信じにくいこともあります。
Embarrassment can make someone defend the mistake longer.恥ずかしさのため、間違いを長く守ってしまうことがあります。
A good correction provides evidence and room to change calmly.よい訂正には、根拠と落ち着いて変わる余地があります。
Finding one mistake should lead to better checking, not certainty that everything else is wrong.一つの間違いを見つけたら、ほかもすべて間違いだと決めつけず、よりよく確かめるべきです。
People can learn to state how sure they are and why.どのくらい確かで、なぜそう思うかを言えるようになります。
Changing a belief is a strength when evidence changes.根拠が変わったときに考えを変えるのは強さです。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of the conversation.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。会話の中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Use these pages as a flexible pool. Ask one question, react naturally, and share a brief real answer of your own when it adds to the exchange. Skip freely and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Completely wrong (完全な思い違い)

WARM-UP 1What's a belief that many people once accepted but later proved wrong?多くの人が以前は信じていたものの、後に間違いだと分かった考えは何ですか？

Follow up
  • Choose one belief. Why did it seem true?
  • How confident were people?

The old idea (以前の考え)

WARM-UP 2What exactly did the person believe?その人は正確には何を信じていましたか？

Follow up
  • Was the belief specific?
  • What did it explain?

How long (信じた期間)

QUESTION 1How long did the person believe it, or how widely was it believed?その人はどのくらい信じていましたか、それとも、どのくらい広く信じられていましたか？

Follow up
  • Did confidence grow?
  • Was it ever questioned?

Where it came from (情報源)

QUESTION 2Where did the belief come from?その考えはどこから来ましたか？

Follow up
  • Was the source trusted?
  • Was the original source remembered?

Finding out (間違いに気づく)

QUESTION 3How did the person find out it was wrong?その人は、どうやって間違いだと知りましたか？

Follow up
  • What evidence mattered?
  • Was the correction immediate?

The first reaction (最初の反応)

QUESTION 4Did the person accept the correction, argue, or need time?その人は訂正を受け入れましたか、反論しましたか、それとも時間が必要でしたか？

Follow up
  • What supported acceptance, resistance, or delay?
  • Was embarrassment involved, or was another concern more important?

What changed after (その後の変化)

QUESTION 5What changed after the correction?訂正の後、何が変わりましたか？

Follow up
  • Which habit or judgment shifted?
  • Why did the correction matter?

What are you sure about? (今確かなこと)

QUESTION 6What are you sure about now, and what supports that confidence?今、何を確かだと思い、その自信を何が支えていますか？

Follow up
  • How strong is the evidence?
  • What could change your mind?

Today's feedback (今日のフィードバック)

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
