Let's talk about why some advice stays with people and deserves to be shared. Please read the title aloud.心に残る助言と、それを人に伝える価値について話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

Why some advice stays (助言が心に残る理由)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Advice often stays because of when we heard it, not only what it said.助言は内容だけでなく、聞いたタイミングによって心に残ります。
A simple sentence can land differently during a difficult decision.難しい決断のときには、簡単な一言も違って響きます。
The speaker matters when we trust their experience or intentions.相手の経験や意図を信頼していると、その人の言葉は大切になります。
We may understand the words immediately but need years to understand their value.言葉はすぐ分かっても、その価値を理解するまで何年もかかることがあります。
Good advice usually leaves room for the listener's real situation.よい助言は、聞く人の実際の状況に合わせる余地があります。
Bad advice may sound confident while ignoring cost, risk, or personality.悪い助言は自信があるように聞こえても、費用や危険、性格を無視することがあります。
Ignoring advice does not always mean it was wrong.助言に従わなくても、その助言が間違っていたとは限りません。
Following advice also does not prove it caused the result.助言に従ったからといって、それが結果の原因だとは限りません。
When we pass advice on, we also pass our interpretation of it.助言を人に伝えるとき、自分なりの解釈も一緒に伝えます。
The best test is whether the advice helps someone see a choice more clearly.よい助言かどうかは、誰かが選択をはっきり見られるようになるかで分かります。

Conversation style (会話スタイル)

Please choose your preferred discussion style.希望する会話の進め方を選んでください。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

Memorable advice (心に残る助言)

WARM-UP 1What's one piece of advice you still remember?今でも覚えている助言を一つ挙げるとしたら、何ですか？

Follow up
  • Was it about work, school, relationships, or daily life?
  • If none comes to mind, what common advice have you heard often?

The exact words (覚えている言葉)

WARM-UP 2What exact words do you remember?どんな言葉だったか、具体的に覚えていますか？

Follow up
  • If not, what was the main idea?
  • Why was the wording easy to remember?

The person behind it (助言をくれた人)

QUESTION 1Who gave you that advice?その助言は、誰からもらいましたか？

Follow up
  • Why did you trust that person?
  • Would the same advice feel different from someone else?

When it made sense (納得できたとき)

QUESTION 2When did the advice start to make sense?その助言が納得できるようになったのは、いつですか？

Follow up
  • Did it arrive before you were ready?
  • What changed later?

Advice today (今の自分への助言)

QUESTION 3Would you still follow that advice today?今でも、その助言に従いますか？

Follow up
  • Which part still fits?
  • What has changed?

Its limits (助言の限界)

QUESTION 4When might that advice stop being useful?その助言が役に立たなくなるのは、どんなときですか？

Follow up
  • Which situation would not fit it?
  • Could the advice be adjusted?

Passing it on (助言を伝えること)

QUESTION 5Would you pass it on to someone else?その助言を、ほかの人にも伝えたいですか？

Follow up
  • Who might benefit from it?
  • How could you share it without sounding controlling?

Advice you reject (賛成できない助言)

QUESTION 6Now the opposite: what's a common piece of advice you don't agree with?では逆に、よく聞くけれど賛成できない助言は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why does it not work for everyone?
  • Could it still help in one situation?

Today's feedback (今日のフィードバック)

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
