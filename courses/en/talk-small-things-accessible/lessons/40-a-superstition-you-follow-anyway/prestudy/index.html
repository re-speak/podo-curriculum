A superstition you follow anyway (信じていなくても従う迷信)

Let's talk about why superstitions survive even when people doubt them. Please read the title aloud.人が疑っていても迷信が残る理由について話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

Belief, habit, and luck (信念、習慣、運)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
A superstition connects an action or sign with luck without clear proof.迷信は、はっきりした証拠なしに、行動やしるしと運を結びつけます。
People can follow one without fully believing it.人は、完全に信じていなくても迷信に従うことがあります。
The action may be easy, while ignoring it feels strangely risky.従うのは簡単でも、無視すると妙に不安に感じます。
Many superstitions are learned from family, friends, or school.多くの迷信は、家族や友人、学校から学びます。
Some began as practical warnings that lost their explanation.実用的な注意から始まり、理由だけが忘れられたものもあります。
Others survive because the story is memorable and easy to repeat.印象的で繰り返しやすい話だから残るものもあります。
Breaking a superstition can feel like testing luck on purpose.迷信を破ることは、わざと運を試すように感じられます。
Different places give the same number, animal, or object different meanings.同じ数字や動物、物でも、場所によって意味が違います。
Learning the story matters more than laughing at the rule.その決まりを笑うより、背景の話を知るほうが大切です。
Even a skeptic may keep one ritual because it feels familiar.迷信を疑う人でも、なじみがあるという理由で一つの習慣を続けることがあります。skeptic疑う人

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

A familiar superstition (よく知る迷信)

WARM-UP 1Which superstition do you know best, whether or not you follow it?従っているかどうかに関係なく、いちばんよく知っている迷信は何ですか？

Follow up
  • What action or sign is involved?
  • Which do you know better: the number 13, breaking a mirror, or a lucky charm?

What should happen (起きるとされること)

WARM-UP 2What good or bad thing is a superstition supposed to cause?迷信は、どんなよいことや悪いことを起こすと言われていますか？

Follow up
  • Which result sounds most dramatic?
  • Who usually teaches people the superstition?

Why it survives (残る理由)

QUESTION 1Why do people keep following a superstition they doubt?人は、疑っている迷信にもなぜ従い続けるのでしょうか？

Follow up
  • What feeling makes the habit hard to drop?
  • How can family or tradition matter?

A convincing coincidence (本物に思えた偶然)

QUESTION 2Has a coincidence ever made a superstition feel true?偶然の出来事で、迷信が本当に思えたことはありますか？

Follow up
  • If not, what kind of coincidence might do that?
  • Would you still call it chance?

Break it on purpose (わざと破る)

QUESTION 3What would make you break a superstition on purpose?どのような理由があれば、迷信をわざと破りますか？

Follow up
  • Would any uneasy feeling remain?
  • What new information would change your mind?

A Japanese example (日本の例)

QUESTION 4What's a Japanese superstition an outsider might not know?外国の人が知らなさそうな日本の迷信は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Where might an outsider see it?
  • Which is harder to explain: avoiding four, hiding your thumbs, or not cutting nails at night?

The strangest one (いちばん不思議なもの)

QUESTION 5What's the strangest superstition you've heard of?知っている中で、いちばん不思議な迷信は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Which detail makes it memorable?
  • Which sounds strangest: a broken mirror, an itchy palm, or a black cat?

One to keep (残したいもの)

QUESTION 6Which superstition is worth keeping as a tradition?どの迷信は、伝統として残す価値がありますか？

Follow up
  • How could children learn about it?
  • Which could be a harmless tradition: a lucky charm, a lucky food, or a lucky number?

Today's feedback (今日のフィードバック)

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
