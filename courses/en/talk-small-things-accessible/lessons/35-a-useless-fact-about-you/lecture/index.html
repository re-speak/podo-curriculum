SMALL THINGS

A useless fact about you (自分についての役に立たない事実)

Let's talk about facts that are interesting even when they aren't useful. Please read the title aloud.役に立たなくても面白い事実について話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

The value of useless facts (役に立たない事実の価値)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Personal trivia does not help with work or daily tasks.役に立たない事実は、仕事や日常の用事には役立ちません。
It can still make a conversation more personal and memorable.それでも、会話を個人的で印象に残るものにできます。
Small facts often reveal habits, tastes, or old interests.小さな事実から、習慣や好み、昔の興味が見えることがあります。
Some personal facts are about skills that were useful once but no longer matter.昔は役に立ったものの、今は必要なくなった技能についての事実もあります。
Others come from a coincidence we happened to remember.たまたま覚えていた偶然から生まれるものもあります。
The best useless facts are harmless and easy to explain.よい役に立たない事実は、害がなく、簡単に説明できます。
They give other people an easy way to ask a follow-up question.そのような事実があると、相手も続けて質問しやすくなります。
A fact can feel useless to us but interesting to someone else.自分には役に立たなくても、ほかの人には面白い場合があります。
Useful facts tell people what we can do; useless ones show another side.役に立つ事実はできることを伝え、役に立たない事実は別の一面を見せます。
One odd detail can make an ordinary introduction feel real.少し変わった一つの細部で、普通の自己紹介が自然に感じられます。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

One useless fact (役に立たない事実)

WARM-UP 1What's one fact about you that's interesting but not useful?自分について、面白いけれど役には立たない事実は何ですか？

Follow up
  • How did you discover it?
  • What question might it make someone ask?

Since when (いつから？)

WARM-UP 2How long has that fact been true?その事実は、いつから当てはまりますか？

Follow up
  • Did it happen by accident or by choice?
  • What was different before?

Who would be surprised (驚きそうな人)

QUESTION 1Who would be most surprised by that fact?その事実を知って、いちばん驚きそうな人は誰ですか？

Follow up
  • What would they guess instead?
  • Why has the fact never come up?

Why useless (役に立たない理由)

QUESTION 2What makes the fact useless?その事実は、なぜ役に立たないのですか？

Follow up
  • Could it help in one unusual situation?
  • Why is it worth remembering anyway?

How it became true (そうなった経緯)

QUESTION 3How might an unusual fact about you come about?あなたについての少し変わった事実は、どんな経緯で生まれそうですか？

Follow up
  • Could one turning point create it?
  • Which part of your story would explain it best?

A useful fact (役に立つ事実)

QUESTION 4What's one genuinely useful fact about you?自分について、本当に役に立つ事実は何ですか？

Follow up
  • When does it become useful?
  • Who benefits from knowing it?

A conversation starter (会話のきっかけ)

QUESTION 5Which useless facts make the best conversation starters?どのような役に立たない事実が、会話のよいきっかけになりますか？

Follow up
  • What makes one easy to ask about?
  • When would a fact feel too personal?

A playful introduction (楽しい自己紹介)

QUESTION 6How would you use an unusual fact about yourself in a playful introduction?自分についての少し変わった事実を、楽しい自己紹介でどう使いますか？

Follow up
  • What exact sentence would you say?
  • What follow-up would keep the conversation going?

Today's feedback (今日のフィードバック)

Before we finish, here's one strength and one thing to focus on next.最後に、よかった点を一つと、次に意識する点を一つ伝えます。

Before closing, ask the learner to repeat one useful correction.
