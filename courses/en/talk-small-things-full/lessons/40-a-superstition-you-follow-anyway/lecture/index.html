Belief, habit, and luck (信念、習慣、運)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
A superstition links an action, object, or sign to an outcome without reliable evidence.迷信は、確かな根拠なしに、行動や物、しるしを結果と結びつけます。
People may follow a superstition even while saying they do not believe what it predicts.迷信が予告することは信じないと言いながらも、それに従う人はいます。
The ritual may cost almost nothing, while breaking it creates a small but persistent discomfort.その行動にはほとんど負担がない一方で、破ると小さいながら消えない不安が生まれます。ritual決まった行動
Superstitions often travel through families, peer groups, workplaces, and childhood warnings.迷信は、家庭、友人関係、職場、子どものころの注意を通して伝わることがよくあります。
Some may preserve an old practical warning after its original explanation has disappeared.もともとの説明が消えたあとも、昔の実用的な注意だけを残しているものもあります。
Others survive because a vivid story is easier to transmit than a careful explanation.丁寧な説明より鮮明な物語のほうが伝えやすいため、残っているものもあります。
Deliberately breaking a superstition can feel like volunteering for an unnecessary test of luck.迷信を意図的に破ることは、必要もないのに自分から運試しをするように感じられます。
The same number, animal, or household object can carry opposite meanings across cultures.同じ数字、動物、家庭用品でも、文化によって正反対の意味を持つことがあります。
Understanding the social story is usually more interesting than judging whether the rule is rational.その決まりが合理的かを判断するより、社会的な背景を理解するほうがたいてい面白いものです。
Even a committed skeptic may preserve one ritual because familiarity can be emotionally reassuring.強く疑う人でも、なじみが心を落ち着かせるため、一つの習慣を残すことがあります。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

A familiar superstition (よく知る迷信)

WARM-UP 1Which superstition do you know best, whether or not you follow it?従っているかどうかに関係なく、いちばんよく知っている迷信は何ですか？

Follow up
  • What action or sign is involved?
  • Which do you know better: the number 13, breaking a mirror, or a lucky charm?

What should happen (起きるとされること)

WARM-UP 2What good or bad thing is a superstition supposed to cause?迷信は、どんなよいことや悪いことを起こすと言われていますか？

Follow up
  • Which result sounds most dramatic?
  • Who usually teaches people the superstition?

Why it survives (残る理由)

QUESTION 1Why do people keep following a superstition they doubt?人は、疑っている迷信にもなぜ従い続けるのでしょうか？

Follow up
  • What feeling makes the habit hard to drop?
  • How can family or tradition matter?

A believable part (信じられる部分)

QUESTION 2What part of a superstition can feel believable even when you doubt it?疑っていても、迷信のどんな部分は信じられそうに感じますか？

Follow up
  • Could coincidence strengthen the belief?
  • Might the rule contain practical wisdom?

Break it on purpose (わざと破る)

QUESTION 3What would make you break a superstition on purpose?どのような理由があれば、迷信をわざと破りますか？

Follow up
  • Would any uneasy feeling remain?
  • What new information would change your mind?

A Japanese example (日本の例)

QUESTION 4What's a Japanese superstition an outsider might not know?外国の人が知らなさそうな日本の迷信は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Where might an outsider see it?
  • Which is harder to explain: avoiding four, hiding your thumbs, or not cutting nails at night?

The strangest one (いちばん不思議なもの)

QUESTION 5What's the strangest superstition you've heard of?知っている中で、いちばん不思議な迷信は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Which detail makes it memorable?
  • Which sounds strangest: a broken mirror, an itchy palm, or a black cat?

One to keep (残したいもの)

QUESTION 6Which superstition is worth keeping as a tradition?どの迷信は、伝統として残す価値がありますか？

Follow up
  • How could children learn about it?
  • Which could be a harmless tradition: a lucky charm, a lucky food, or a lucky number?
