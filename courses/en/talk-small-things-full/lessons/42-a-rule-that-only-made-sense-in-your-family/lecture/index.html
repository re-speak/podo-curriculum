A rule that only made sense in your family (自分の家でだけ通じた決まり)

Let's talk about why household rules make sense in one home and seem strange in another. Please read the title aloud.家庭の決まりが、ある家では自然で別の家では不思議に見える理由について話しましょう。タイトルを声に出して読んでください。

Have the learner read the title once, then move on.

Every home makes a small culture (どの家庭にも小さな文化がある)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
Every household develops rules that appear self-evident to the people living within it.どの家庭にも、そこで暮らす人には自明に見える決まりが生まれます。
Some rules respond to a practical constraint that is invisible outside the household.家庭の外からは見えない実際的な制約に対応する決まりもあります。
Other rules survive as habits long after their original purpose has vanished.別の決まりは、最初の目的が消えたあとも習慣として残ります。
Children naturally mistake household customs for universal rules.子どもは家庭の習慣を、誰にでも共通する決まりだと思い込みがちです。
One visit, meal, or sleepover can suddenly expose how local a familiar rule is.一度の訪問や食事、泊まりで、慣れた決まりがどれほどその家だけのものか突然分かります。
Discovering the difference can produce amusement, embarrassment, defensiveness, or relief.違いに気づくと、面白さ、恥ずかしさ、反発、安心などが生まれることがあります。
We may reproduce a household rule automatically even after rejecting the logic behind it.背景の理屈を否定したあとでも、家庭の決まりを無意識に再現することがあります。
A rule that seems bizarre elsewhere may become entirely reasonable once its context is known.ほかでは奇妙に見える決まりでも、その背景が分かれば完全に合理的になることがあります。
Carrying a rule into the next household is a deliberate choice rather than an inherited obligation.決まりを次の家庭へ持ち込むことは、受け継いだ義務ではなく、意識的な選択です。inherited受け継いだ
The rules retold years later become part of the story a family tells about itself.何年後も語られる決まりは、その家族が自分たちについて語る物語の一部になります。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

A household rule (家庭の決まり)

WARM-UP 1Which house rule matters most: taking off your shoes, keeping quiet, or cleaning up?家のルールでは、靴を脱ぐこと、静かにすること、片づけることのどれが最も大切ですか？

Follow up
  • What problem might it solve?
  • Why might an outsider misunderstand it?

How it works (決まりの内容)

WARM-UP 2How would an unusual household rule work day to day?家庭の変わった決まりは、日常の中でどのように使われますか？

Follow up
  • Who would be expected to follow it?
  • What might happen when someone forgets?

Discovering differences (違いに気づく時)

QUESTION 1How do people discover that other households have different rules?人は、ほかの家庭に違う決まりがあると、どのように気づくのでしょうか？

Follow up
  • Which situation reveals the difference fastest?
  • What reaction might follow?

Understanding the rule (決まりを理解する)

QUESTION 2What makes an unfamiliar household rule feel reasonable?知らない家庭の決まりが、納得できるようになるのはどのようなときですか？

Follow up
  • Which missing detail matters most?
  • What value might the rule protect?

Worth keeping (残す価値)

QUESTION 3Which family rules are worth keeping as an adult?どのような家庭の決まりは、大人になっても残す価値がありますか？

Follow up
  • What purpose do they still serve?
  • How might they need updating?

Where rules differ (決まりが違う分野)

QUESTION 4Which causes more house rules: meals, cleaning, noise, or privacy?食事、掃除、騒音、プライバシーでは、どれが最も多くの家のルールを生みますか？

Follow up
  • Why does that area need rules?
  • Which rule could cause an argument?

Explaining it (説明の仕方)

QUESTION 5How should a household explain a rule to a guest?家庭の決まりを、来客にどのように説明するとよいですか？

Follow up
  • Which rule needs advance warning?
  • Where should the household stay flexible?

Known for (覚えられる決まり)

QUESTION 6What small house rule would you recommend to other families?ほかの家庭にすすめたい小さな家のルールは何ですか？

Follow up
  • What problem would it solve?
  • When should the rule be flexible?
