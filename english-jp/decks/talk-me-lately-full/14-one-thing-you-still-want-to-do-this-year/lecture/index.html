Turning a wish into a plan (願いを計画に変える)

Did you have any questions about the article?記事について何か質問はありましたか？

Answer the learner's questions, then move on. If there are none—or the learner did not complete the pre-study—skip the article; do not spend class time reading it.
A deadline can turn a vague wish into a decision that competes for real time.期限があると、ぼんやりした願いが、実際の時間を使う決断に変わります。
Choosing one priority also means accepting that several worthwhile goals may have to wait.目標を一つ選ぶことは、ほかの目標を待たせることでもあります。
We often postpone a goal because the first action is ambiguous, uncomfortable, or too large.最初の行動が曖昧だったり、不快だったり、大きすぎたりすると、目標を先延ばしにしがちです。
Breaking the goal into a small, observable action reduces the effort needed to begin.小さな行動に分けると、始めやすくなります。
A lack of time is not always the real barrier; fear, uncertainty, and possible judgment also matter.本当の障害は時間不足とは限らず、不安や自信のなさ、人の評価への恐れも関係します。
Consciously releasing an outdated goal can create room for a priority that still fits our identity.今の自分らしさに合わない古い目標を意識して手放すと、今も大切なことのための余裕ができます。
Sharing a goal can create useful accountability, although it may also create unwanted pressure.目標を人に話すと、やり遂げる責任感が生まれますが、望まないプレッシャーにもなりえます。accountabilityやり遂げる責任感
The final result may matter less than learning to take the next step consistently.最終結果よりも、次の一歩を着実に続けられるようになることが大切な場合もあります。
One concrete action this week is usually more valuable than a perfect plan that begins next month.来月からの完璧な計画より、今週の具体的な一歩のほうが役立ちます。
A serious goal can still be revised as new information changes what is possible or worthwhile.新しい情報によって可能なことや価値のあることが変われば、本気の目標でも見直せます。

8 QUESTION POOL

Let's talk (話そう)

We don't need to answer every question. Let's follow the most interesting parts of your answers.全部の質問に答える必要はありません。答えの中でいちばん面白いところを広げていきましょう。

Treat these pages as a pool, not a sequence. React or share briefly before choosing the next prompt, skip freely, and move to feedback with 2–3 minutes left.

One goal (一つの目標)

WARM-UP 1What's one thing you'd like to finish this year?今年中に終わらせたいことを一つ挙げるとしたら何ですか？

Follow up
  • What would completing it change?
  • What might you need to put aside?

One unfinished thing (やり残していること)

WARM-UP 2If you could finish one unfinished thing this month, what would you choose?今月、やり残していることを一つ終えられるなら、何を選びますか？

Follow up
  • Why would you choose that first?
  • How much time would it need?

The biggest difference (いちばん大きな変化)

QUESTION 1Which goal would make the biggest difference to your everyday life this year?今年、日常生活にいちばん大きな変化をもたらしそうな目標は何ですか？

Follow up
  • What would change first?
  • What makes the goal worth your time?

What gets in the way (進めない理由)

QUESTION 2Which gets in the way more: time, money, or doubt?目標の邪魔になりやすいのは、時間、お金、自信のなさのどれですか？

Follow up
  • How does that problem usually appear?
  • What small step could help?

What you dropped (あきらめたこと)

QUESTION 3When is dropping a goal a smart decision?目標をやめることが賢い判断になるのは、どんなときですか？

Follow up
  • How do you know it is time to stop trying?
  • Is stopping always the same as failing?

Who would notice (気づく人)

QUESTION 4How can sharing a goal make success feel different?目標を誰かに話すと、達成したときの気持ちはどう変わりますか？

Follow up
  • Who, if anyone, would understand the effort?
  • Would you share it with one person or keep it private?

A realistic limit (現実的な限界)

QUESTION 5What kind of goal is okay to change as life changes?生活が変わったとき、どんな目標なら変えてもよいと思いますか？

Follow up
  • What should stay important?
  • Is it okay to make a goal smaller when life gets busy?

This week's step (今週の一歩)

QUESTION 6What's one useful first step toward a difficult goal?難しい目標に向かう、役に立つ最初の一歩は何ですか？

Follow up
  • Why is that step small enough to start?
  • What would show that it worked?
