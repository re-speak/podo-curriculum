## The three moves worth stealing

**1. The slot is marked the same way in both languages.** On p.30 the phrase is
`好きな[アルバム]は何ですか？` over `좋아하는 [앨범] 뭐예요?` — and the swappable word sits in an
identical dashed pink box in *both* lines. The learner never has to work out which word is the
variable; the two lines point at each other. Our `#p-curri` stage-2 demo does the same thing
with the lime `.wrd.hole`, but only on the Korean line. **Marking the Japanese gloss the same
way is the missing half.**

**2. A frame plus a bank, not a list of finished sentences.** Every slot phrase is immediately
followed by a "Let's try" bank of ~12 nouns to drop into it. The page sells leverage: one frame
× twelve words. p.124 pushes this furthest — two colour-coded columns, 誰と (yellow) and どうする
(pink), pick one from each. Twelve cards, thirty-six sentences. That is a very high
value-per-page ratio and it is already tap-shaped: two columns, one selection each.

**3. The word cell has exactly three layers, always in the same order.**

```
        カス          ← katakana reading, small, ABOVE
        가수          ← Korean, big, black
        歌手          ← Japanese meaning, small, pink, BELOW
```

Reading above, meaning below, never mixed. It scans as one object at a glance. Our word chips
put the meaning inline (`카페 カフェ`), which is fine at chip size but doesn't hold up in a grid.

## What NOT to copy

**The furigana-on-everything convention.** Every Korean word in this book carries a katakana
reading, so the learner never has to decode Hangul — they can use the whole book without
reading a single letter. That's correct for a phrasebook and **directly opposed to
`1-hangul`**, whose entire job is to remove that crutch. Kana anchors belong on *letters being
taught*, not on every word forever. See `ux-philosophy.md` → "Romanize the parts".

**The mascot asides.** Cute, and they do carry real trivia (`略して룸메と言おう!`), but they are
floating speech bubbles with no fixed slot, so the grid reads ragged. If we want the same
warmth, it has to sit in a defined column — see the "every slot in a repeated row is filled"
rule.

**No production step.** The book is browse-and-recognize throughout; nothing ever asks the
learner to produce a line unaided. Our arc has to keep going receptive → productive.
