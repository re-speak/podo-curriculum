# Proposed Authoritative Curriculum-Build Method

> **STATUS: PROPOSED — NOT YET APPLIED TO THE CURRENT CURRICULUM**
>
> This document describes the evidence-first method we intend to use for building or fully revalidating the English curriculum. It is **not** a description of how the existing 72-lesson Core was originally created. The current Core and grammar coverage map have not yet completed the row-by-row source audit required below.

## Why this method exists

The current curriculum was created pattern-first: communicative situations and speaking frames were designed first, and the grammar coverage map was backfilled afterward to expose dependencies and gaps. That produced a useful working curriculum, but it did not give every grammar decision a traceable authoritative source.

This proposed method keeps the pattern-first teaching philosophy while adding evidence before finalizing scope, level, and order. It is intended to reduce omissions, arbitrary sequencing, and AI- or author-generated assumptions.

## Source hierarchy

### 1. Council of Europe CEFR and Companion Volume

**Authority for:** communicative outcomes, can-do descriptors, language activities, interaction, mediation, performance quality, and broad level claims.

- [Council of Europe — CEFR framework](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/introduction-and-context)
- [Council of Europe — CEFR descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors)
- [Council of Europe — purposes and curriculum use](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/uses-and-objectives)
- [CEFR Companion Volume (2020)](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)

CEFR is authoritative but language-neutral and non-prescriptive. It does not provide an official English grammar syllabus or lesson order.

### 2. English Reference Level Descriptions

**Authority for:** the principle that language-neutral CEFR competence must be translated into English-specific vocabulary, grammar, and exponents.

- [Council of Europe — Reference Level Descriptions by language](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/reference-level-descriptions)

These descriptions are recognized within the CEFR framework but produced by expert national or institutional teams rather than directly by the Council of Europe.

### 3. EAQUALS/British Council Core Inventory for General English

**Primary practical baseline for:** English functions, grammar, discourse markers, topics, and approximate CEFR teaching bands for adult General English.

- [EAQUALS — Core Inventory resource page](https://www.eaquals.org/resources/the-core-inventory-for-general-english/)
- [EAQUALS/British Council — Core Inventory PDF](https://www.eaquals.org/wp-content/uploads/EAQUALS_British_Council_Core_Curriculum_April2011.pdf)

The Inventory combines analysis of CEFR descriptors, established CEFR-aligned syllabuses, widely used coursebooks, teacher surveys, and examination-board input. It is guidance rather than a mandatory syllabus.

### 4. Cambridge English Profile and English Grammar Profile

**Corpus-informed cross-check for:** the particular forms, meanings, and uses that learners competently produce at different CEFR levels.

- [Cambridge — English Profile programme](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/english-profile-journal)
- [Cambridge — how the English Grammar Profile was built](https://www.cambridge.org/elt/blog/2015/11/11/introducing-english-grammar-profile-1-building-profile/)
- [Cambridge — how to use the English Grammar Profile](https://www.cambridge.org/elt/blog/2015/11/16/introducing-english-grammar-profile-2-whats-use-egp/)
- [Cambridge — criterial features for grammar instruction](https://www.cambridge.org/elt/blog/2021/06/23/using-cefr-criterial-features-for-grammar-instruction/)

The English Grammar Profile is evidence, not a prescribed syllabus. Its corpus is also weighted toward examination writing, so spoken readiness must be validated separately.

### 5. Spoken-language and lexical evidence

**Evidence for:** whether candidate speaking patterns are natural, frequent, retrievable, and useful.

- [British Council — lexical approach](https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/teaching-knowledge-database/d-h/lexical-approach)
- Reputable learner dictionaries and corpus-informed spoken-English references
- Established adult General English series used as coverage benchmarks, not copied sources
- Recorded learner performance and delayed-retrieval data from our own lessons

## Proposed build process

### Step 1. Define the learner and endpoint

Record the target learner's age, first language, speaking needs, expected English variety, lesson count, and intended endpoint. Do not interpret “B1” as one universal course without this context.

### Step 2. Select communicative outcomes from CEFR

Build the initial scope from relevant CEFR can-do descriptors and real learner needs. Translate broad descriptors into observable course outcomes such as exchanging personal information, completing a transaction, telling a short story, explaining a problem, or negotiating a familiar solution.

**Output:** a can-do inventory with a CEFR descriptor or scale reference for every outcome.

### Step 3. Establish the English content baseline

For each can-do, consult the Core Inventory and record the functions, grammar, discourse markers, and vocabulary areas commonly associated with its level. Also scan the whole target range for important items not naturally surfaced by the first can-do list.

**Output:** a coverage matrix showing what is included, deferred, or intentionally excluded.

### Step 4. Check particular grammar uses in the English Grammar Profile

Do not assign one level to a broad label such as “reported speech” or “present perfect.” Identify the particular form, meaning, and use required by the intended speaking pattern, then record the English Grammar Profile evidence where available.

**Output:** a form-and-meaning evidence row, not merely a grammar-topic label.

### Step 5. Write candidate speaking patterns

Choose retrievable frames that accomplish the can-do. Each pattern must pass the level-sensitive test in [`teaching-philosophy.md`](./teaching-philosophy.md): stable purpose, clear boundaries, natural range, useful transfer, and appropriate slot load.

Fixed chunks remain supporting expressions. Grammar explanations remain support for the two production targets.

**Output:** at least three candidate pairs before selecting the final two.

### Step 6. Check spoken naturalness and usefulness

Check candidate frames against reliable corpus-informed references, learner dictionaries, and representative contemporary usage. Reject frames that are technically grammatical but unusually formal, narrow, or unlikely in the target situation.

**Output:** a short naturalness note and several attested or independently verified substitutions.

### Step 7. Build the dependency graph

Record what the learner must already control inside every slot: vocabulary type, inflection, clause structure, interactional convention, and processing load. Order lessons so productive dependencies point backward.

An earlier bounded lexical chunk is allowed only when it is explicitly labeled as a preview and is not counted as mastery of the full grammar system.

**Output:** an acyclic lesson dependency graph.

### Step 8. Pair patterns into cumulative lessons and units

Each lesson receives one can-do and two cooperating patterns. Each unit culminates in a task that naturally retrieves its lessons. Contextual English must reuse a Core frame exactly or make a documented bounded expansion. Freetalking must name the Core threshold for every topic.

**Output:** Core, Contextual, and Freetalking maps with explicit recycling links.

### Step 9. Run a source and difficulty audit

Audit every grammar-map row and lesson for:

- CEFR outcome evidence;
- Core Inventory scope and level;
- English Grammar Profile form-and-meaning evidence;
- productive, lexical-preview, or receptive status;
- prerequisite integrity;
- pattern changeability and retrieval;
- unit coherence;
- downstream recycling.

When credible sources disagree, use the more conservative band for independent production unless there is a documented reason not to.

### Step 10. Pilot and revise

Textual authority cannot prove teachability. Pilot representative lessons, record task performance, test delayed retrieval, and observe whether learners transfer patterns to new details. Revise pairs that cause competition, hidden grammar overload, or weak transfer.

**Output:** evidence-backed changes and a dated validation record.

## Required traceability record

Before this method can be called implemented, every Core lesson should have an author-only record containing:

| Field | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Can-do | CEFR descriptor or clearly documented learner need |
| Function | Core Inventory function or documented rationale |
| Grammar use | Specific form and meaning, not only a broad label |
| Level evidence | Core Inventory band plus English Grammar Profile result where available |
| Pattern evidence | Natural model and at least four viable substitutions |
| Prerequisites | Earlier lessons required to build the slots |
| Status | productive target, bounded lexical preview, or receptive support |
| Recycling | later Contextual and Freetalking uses |
| Validation | pilot result and delayed-retrieval result |

## What has and has not been done

### Already done

- A 72-lesson, two-pattern Core exists.
- Contextual and Freetalking tracks exist.
- A retrospective grammar and dependency map exists.
- Fixed expressions have been separated from primary patterns.
- Clause-slot complexity and Freetalking entry points have been reviewed.
- The curriculum now documents its source hierarchy honestly.

### Not yet done

- The 72 lessons have **not** been rebuilt from this evidence-first process.
- The grammar-map rows do **not** yet contain exact Core Inventory and English Grammar Profile citations.
- Every can-do has **not** yet been linked to a specific CEFR descriptor.
- Candidate patterns have **not** yet received systematic corpus evidence.
- The B1+ bridge has **not** yet been fully reconciled with the English-specific sources.
- The sequence has **not** yet been validated through recorded learner pilots and delayed retrieval.

Until those steps are complete, the present curriculum is a thoughtful working design—not a fully source-audited CEFR-aligned curriculum.
