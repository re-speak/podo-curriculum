## What this track sells — a scene, not a topic

Nobody arrives here to study English. They arrive because they are about to move abroad, because
their company just merged with an overseas team, because they have a trip booked, or because there
is someone at their climbing gym they cannot talk to yet. So the success condition is one thing:
**"I am inside a situation I recognise, and I have the lines for it."**

The grammar gets pulled out of the scene. **The opposite direction is this track's failure mode** —
teaching a form and then dressing an example sentence in travel vocabulary. That produces a grammar
course wearing a costume, and learners can tell.

Which is why episodes are written this way:

- **The scene comes first.** Every episode opens with a `場面:` line — **who, where, what is
  happening.** Without it the lines become example sentences and the deck writer invents a situation
  on the spot.
- **Every line has someone on the other end.** The learner's line is followed by `→` and the
  partner's real reaction. Half of what makes a scene work is the reply, and two lines spoken into
  the air are not a script however well written.
- **A show is one work.** Each has a title, a cast, a relationship that changes, and episodes
  numbered in order. These were eight lists of situations before; now they are stories, which is
  what makes episode 6 land differently from episode 1.
- **The learner is cast, not seated.** The speaker of every learner line is `私`. The tutor reads
  the other parts.

**Grammar's home is still `1-core-patterns`.** This track is where the learner *uses* it inside
something they care about, so the grammar difficulty inside a season can be uneven — a real argument
needs `if anything` and a real apology needs `I should have`, and removing them to protect a
sequence would remove the scene.

## The five regions of an episode

| Region | What | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ***場面*** | who · where · what is happening | the deck's starting point — the screen comes from this line |
| **Can-do** | the one thing the learner can do afterwards | the pass condition |
| **2 lines + 2 patterns** | the body. After `→` is the partner's reaction | **production** — said without the model |
| ***Expressions*** | collocations and fixed chunks the scene needs | **production** — usable in a sentence |
| ***Understand*** | what staff, signs and strangers say back | **reception only** — recognise it, never produce it |

**Two new patterns per episode is the ceiling — that is what 25 minutes holds.** The partner's reply
and everything in *Understand* are reception targets and **do not count** toward the two.

## Core numbers are signposts, not gates

Every pattern carries the Core lesson that owns its rule — `Could you help me with ___?` **(Core 20)**.
That number does **not** mean "finish Core 20 first." It means "the rule for this is taught at Core
20, whenever you get there."

Each show has **one low entry floor**, and each season states the Core progress it assumes. Above
the floor, come in any time.

A season routinely uses Core material a little ahead of its own floor — the show and Core advance in
parallel, and a learner in Season 2 has moved on since Season 1. So **`chunk` does not mark
"anything above the floor"**; that would fire on roughly half of all patterns and stop meaning
anything. It marks a pattern **more than two Core units (about 12 lessons) beyond the season's
floor** — far enough that the learner cannot derive the rule and should simply learn the sentence
whole, meeting the rule later.

Blocking a track that exists to pull people in would leave it as a prize nobody reaches. **Hide
nothing, block nothing.**

Shows are independent of one another, so `(review)` marks apply only **within** a show.

# Show 1 · The Exchange

**Premise.** 私 arrives for a year abroad. The story runs from not knowing how the shower works to
not wanting to leave.

| Cast | Who | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **私** | the learner — an exchange student from Japan | the part the learner plays |
| **Ellie** | housemate, local, warm and very direct | the one who says the unsaid thing |
| **Marcus** | course tutor, patient, slightly formal | the safe adult |
| **Priya** | another international student, from Delhi, already fluent | the mirror — same boat, further along |

**Arc.** S1 *Landing* — everything is admin and nothing is home. S2 *Settling in* — routines form,
and so does the first real friction. S3 *Belonging* — the year is ending and 私 has to say things
that matter.

## Season 1 · Landing · 6 episodes · **A1–A2** · floor: Core 24

**1. The room is fine, but —**

*場面: the shared house, first evening. Ellie is showing 私 the kitchen and talking fast.*
*Can-do: introduce yourself to someone you'll live with, and say one thing you need.*
- 私: "Hi, I'm Yuki. I'm from Osaka." — `Hi, I'm ___. I'm from ___.` **(Core 1, 3)**
  → Ellie: "Yuki! Great. Kettle's there, milk's mine, don't touch the top shelf."
- 私: "Sorry, could you say that again?" — `Sorry, could you say ___ again?` **(Core 24)**
  → Ellie: "Ha — sorry. I talk too fast. The kettle. Over there."
- *Expressions:* `nice to meet you` · `no worries`
- *Understand:* `Make yourself at home.` · `Help yourself.`
- *継続:* Ellie is established as fast and kind. 私 has used the repair line once — it returns in ep 4.

**2. Where do I get one of those?**

*場面: campus, day two. 私 needs a student card and has no idea where to go.*
*Can-do: ask where something is and follow the answer.*
- 私: "Excuse me, where's the student office?" — `Where's the ___?` **(Core 11)**
  → Marcus: "Two floors up. There's a lift behind you."
- 私: "Is there a café near here?" — `Is there a ___ near here?` **(Core 7)**
  → Marcus: "There's one in the library. Better coffee than ours, honestly."
- *Expressions:* `on the second floor` · `you can't miss it`
- *Understand:* `Take the lift to the third.` · `It's just around the corner.`
- *継続:* first contact with Marcus. He answers beyond the question — that is his character.

**3. Two of these, please**

*場面: the campus shop at lunch, a queue behind 私.*
*Can-do: buy several things and check the price without stalling the queue.*

  → Staff: "Two-forty. Four-zero."
- *Expressions:* `card, please` · `keep the receipt`
- *Understand:* `Anything else?` · `Do you need a bag?`
- *継続:* the number-repair line is the one 私 will need every day, so it is drilled early.

**4. It's not working**

*場面: the house, evening. The shower runs cold and Ellie is out.*
*Can-do: report a problem and ask someone to act.*

- *Expressions:* `hot water` · `give it a minute`
- *継続:* the first time 私 asks Ellie for something rather than the other way round.

**5. I usually have breakfast at seven**

*場面: the kitchen, one week in. Priya and 私 work out who uses the kitchen when.*
*Can-do: describe your routine and find where it clashes with someone else's.*

- *Expressions:* `most days` · `it depends`
- *継続:* Priya enters — the one who makes living abroad look easy, which is useful and a little

**6. Are you free on Saturday?**

*場面: end of the first month. Priya is going somewhere and 私 wants to be asked.*
*Can-do: arrange to do something with someone.*

# Show 2 · The 9 A.M. Call

**Premise.** 私 works at a Tokyo company that has just started working with an overseas team. The
calls are at 9 a.m. because that is late evening for the other side, and everyone is tired.

| Cast | Who | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **私** | mid-level, Tokyo office, competent in Japanese and hesitant in English | the part the learner plays |
| **Dana** | counterpart abroad, direct, fast, not unkind | says what she thinks immediately |
| **Kenji** | 私's Japanese manager, cautious | wants things softened; sometimes wrong to |
| **Tom** | Dana's engineer, informal, jokes constantly | the one whose English is hardest to follow |

**Arc.** S1 *The new team* — introductions, and the discovery that directness is not rudeness.
S2 *The project* — deadlines slip and 私 has to deliver bad news. S3 *The decision* — 私 disagrees
with Dana in front of everyone, and is right.

## Season 2 · The project · 6 episodes · **B1–B1+** · floor: Core 70

*場面: the first slip. It is small, and 私 wants to flag it before it is not.*

## How an episode passes

A learner passes by **completing the scene with both patterns**, not by reciting the model. In the
final role-play, change at least two details — the object, the reason, the time, the outcome — so
the frame is doing the work and not the memory.

Reception targets (`→` replies and the *Understand* section) are never assessed as production. A
learner who recognises `Anything to declare?` has passed that part of the episode.
